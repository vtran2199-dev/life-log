# November 7, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

The day after signing with Sparta, Victor explored whether he could turn his JobCRM-style intelligence engine into a small TRI-R consulting business, then went down a surprisingly delightful luxury-fashion rabbit hole and moved a matcha date with Adeline off Tinder and into texting.

## What Happened

### A possible second revenue engine: TRI-R

With Friday's Sparta offer signed and the immediate job-search emergency finally behind him, Victor spent part of Saturday thinking through a separate opportunity with **TRI-R** and **Spencer**. The previous day's Houston market-growth proposal had already been sent; Saturday's work was analysis and commercial planning, **not a new signed engagement or a completed discovery call**.

He explored what TRI-R actually sells, what kinds of facilities would be worthwhile, and how to distinguish a promising industrial account from a giant but irrelevant list of warehouses. In the discussion, TRI-R was described as a roughly **43-year-old business with $12–15 million in annual revenue**. A **Tesla-linked warehouse project in Brookshire**, involving mezzanine, conveyor, and racking work, offered a concrete example of the kind of project and potential follow-on opportunity worth investigating. These were discussion inputs, not newly verified contracts or Saturday sales.

The familiar **JobCRM** framework suddenly looked reusable: discover accounts, collect evidence, qualify signals, prioritize targets, then queue specific actions. Victor considered **Apify, APIs, Google Maps, HubSpot**, industrial-facility signals, and outbound workflows. He also had the amusing realization that **“Apify” begins with “API.”** A review of existing JobCRM architecture helped translate the concepts, but there was **no confirmed Saturday code shipment, deployment, or live prospecting system**.

The more important question became commercial rather than technical: who owns the relationship, what exactly counts as a qualified opportunity, does TRI-R have capacity to service leads, and would Victor have to close deals himself? He explored hypothetical pilot fees and retainers, but **no compensation terms were agreed**. The sensible next milestone was a **30–45-minute Zoom discovery conversation with Spencer**, using a narrow Greater Houston pilot before spending on software or attempting a global market map. One proposed test was to find 100 facilities, substantiate 10 signals, and have Spencer choose five—but that was a proposed experiment, not work completed.

The underlying excitement was bigger than TRI-R alone: he could imagine building repeatable market intelligence that produces independent revenue, without repeating the trap of engineering a giant system before anyone validates the buying motion.

### Fashion shows became unexpectedly good entertainment

Victor was struck by how much he enjoyed **Chanel's theatrical runway productions**. A dark, coliseum-like setting with illuminated planets, and another scene featuring glowing swan- or bird-like forms, felt relaxing and visually stimulating rather than like a conventional clothing presentation. The music and staging mattered as much as the outfits. **Louis Vuitton and Saint Laurent/YSL** had been his usual favorites; Chanel was a surprising addition to the watchlist.

He also went looking for an **Apple-associated fashion show with hot-air balloons**. The search led to **Les Benjamins × Apple, “From Earth to Sky,” in Cappadocia, Turkey**. The day's discovery was that some runway shows work almost like ambient, cinematic live performances—something he could happily watch for the atmosphere alone.

### Adeline: from Tinder to actual texting

The conversation with **Adeline** progressed beyond endless dating-app small talk. She accepted the idea of **matcha** with a simple **“Yes:)”**, said she loved **JTea**, and showed interest in Victor's reading. He wanted to stop circling around the invitation, exchange phone numbers, and arrange something sooner rather than leaving the date vague for another week.

The conversation **moved to texting**, and Adeline sent the exuberant **“HIIII ITS ADELINE”** along with a selfie meme. Victor answered in kind: **“AHAHA SO YOURE A MEME HUH?”** It was a small but memorable change of pace after months dominated by recruiters, interviews, and offer negotiations. A meetup was being discussed; **the matcha date itself had not yet happened**.
