## What Happened

Victor no longer wanted to spend the week interviewing for jobs he would not actually choose over Sparta. He weighed withdrawing from **Zipline**, **Endor Labs**, **Redis** (interview planned for November 10), and **Impact Networking** (VP of Sales interview planned for Monday, November 9). Endor's Austin office requirement and extended interview process were especially unattractive; Impact's product, compensation and onsite demands also failed to excite him. He revisited how far his compensation had come since NetSuite: the old **$50,000 base / $25,000 target commission** versus Sparta's **$70,000 / $40,000**. At full target, that translates to roughly **$6,250 versus $10,000 in quarterly gross commission**, though commission is not guaranteed. The more consequential shift was psychological: after an exhausting search, he could finally prioritize starting a job rather than collecting more interview processes.

### Mercury: an intriguing business without even a CRM

Victor discussed a possible **Mercury** go-to-market role or consulting arrangement involving **Mark and Pranshu**. Victor saw an obvious opening: **Mercury reportedly had no CRM**. He proposed **HubSpot**, organized prospecting, and reusing his JobCRM-style sourcing and intelligence work to import and qualify leads rather than starting from scratch. The economics were not settled. Victor's emerging plan was to **learn HubSpot deeply at Sparta first**, then decide how much Mercury infrastructure and prospecting work he could responsibly undertake; he did not want to personally fund a software subscription. No Mercury CRM implementation, signed partnership, paid project, or code deployment was established on Sunday.
