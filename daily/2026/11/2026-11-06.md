# November 6, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

Friday was the day Victor finally **signed with Sparta**, respectfully closed the Multiview fallback, began imagining the sales systems he could build with real company data, and celebrated with a Whataburger Patty Melt while feeling the exhaustion of a roughly 1,100-application job hunt.

## What Happened

### 6:28 a.m.: the offer became real

Victor electronically signed Sparta Commodities' **Sales Development Representative — Americas** offer at **6:28 a.m. Central**. The terms: **$70,000 base salary, $40,000 target variable compensation ($110,000 OTE), 50 share options**, and a **November 16** start in Houston, reporting to **Isaac Musgrove**. The offer remained subject to normal employment checks and follow-on paperwork. The base had climbed through discussions of $60K and $65K to $70K; Victor still wondered whether he had anchored too low, but the signed outcome was tangible. After a drawn-out search and nearly empty bank account, he was close to tears.

The acceptance-day conversation with Isaac opened up the next chapter: a possible high-spec MacBook Pro, recorded sales calls, HubSpot, Outreach, ZoomInfo, Claude/Claude Code, MCP-connected tools, and freedom to build useful GTM workflows. Isaac introduced **Sam Barnes** in Revenue Operations and Enablement; Victor requested pre-start training, with material moving to **Confluence** after Ascend became unavailable. **Chris Hamil** confirmed the November 16 start and was the interim contact for contract/onboarding matters while Isaac was away. The additional employment documents were still forthcoming; they were not confused with the signed offer.

Victor was thrilled at the idea of entering a mature CRM and prospecting system instead of building a market from nothing. He wanted to listen to calls, study existing accounts, understand territory ownership, and get productive immediately. The commission model was especially compelling: payment for first and second meetings and participation in closed-won revenue, with several BDRs reportedly pacing toward roughly $40K in variable pay. He discussed the larger AE path, including Isaac's reported **$140K base / $300K OTE** AE compensation reference, without treating future promotion or earnings as guaranteed.

### Closing the fallback and cleaning up loose ends

Victor formally **withdrew from Multiview**, whose Irving onsite role was scheduled to start **November 10**. He replied to the existing email thread with a respectful explanation that another opportunity better fit his long-term direction. It felt bad to disappoint Hope and the team, but was clearly better than asking for another extension, ghosting, or failing to appear. The tradeoff was concrete: five-day relocation, a high-volume SMB calling motion, a crowded marketing-services product, and a less attractive path than Sparta's combination of sales and systems work.

He also realized that a **Redis scheduling delay** had come from an availability email he had not sent, rather than a missed interview. He sought Josh Hoffman's availability and sent a correction/rescheduling note to Maya. This was a frustrating but recoverable process error. **Indoor Labs** and **Impact Networking** remained upcoming conversations, not interviews completed Friday. He still wanted closure from Agicap despite its organizational hiring disruption.

Another thread moved forward: Victor prepared and **sent Spencer a TRI-R Houston market-intelligence and revenue-growth proposal PDF** around **5:10 p.m.** It proposed limited consulting or part-time help and a Zoom discussion rather than an in-person visit, with possible availability from Tuesday, November 10. Sending the proposal was the accomplishment; no consulting agreement or reply was confirmed.

### What the paycheck meant

Victor modeled the new salary as **twice-monthly paychecks** and contrasted a $70K base plus quarterly variable compensation with a hypothetical $110K all-base salary. Texas has no state individual income tax, unlike his NetSuite-era California paychecks. He was excited about the prospect of steady income and substantial upside, while recognizing that OTE is a target, not guaranteed cash.

The contrast with his immediate finances was stark: he said he had **less than $50** available and expected roughly **$2,400 by month-end** after getting back on payroll. His parents had helped support him. He reflected on years of trying to create leverage through his own software ventures, including **AdOrder's 108 restaurant visits without a sale**, and the realization that selling an established product through an existing revenue engine could change his situation far faster. The point wasn't that entrepreneurship had been worthless; it was that he no longer had to invent every component of the business just to earn a paycheck.

### Rest, games, and a very large installation

After waking around **3 a.m.**, Victor described his brain as mushy. The emergency job-search mode was finally shutting off after conversations with roughly **35–40 companies**. He had not been coding that day, and found himself playing **Call of Duty** campaign, multiplayer, and zombies without feeling compelled to manufacture another project. He wanted to relax, see friends, return to markets, and eventually build something useful once he understood Sparta's real workflows.

A practical gaming problem turned into an absurd storage audit. Steam showed **Modern Warfare III consuming about 139.33 GB**, including a **46.1 GB campaign** and roughly **31.69 GB multiplayer** package. He wanted to play **Black Ops 6** without abandoning MWIII, but even after unchecking the BO6 campaign the install still called for **96.41 GB**, while C: had only **11.08 GB free**. Removing the MWIII campaign alone would not cover that gap. The investigation explained some of the bloat but did **not** establish that BO6 had been successfully installed or that the disk problem was resolved.

Victor also investigated why a long-running Sparta ChatGPT conversation finally hit the chat-length limit, contrasting it with Codex's context compaction. He considered how to bring his preferred **WhisperKey** dictation, powerful hardware, large monitors, and existing JobCRM/Apify/Clay patterns into the new role. These were ideas and requirements discussions, **not code shipped on Friday**. Later he explored richer account intelligence—hiring signals, trading-desk relationships, commodity exposure, and technographic fingerprints—without claiming a working Sparta system existed yet.

### The Patty Melt victory lap

Victor finally went to **Whataburger** for his beloved **Patty Melt**, something he had not had in a long time. The burger prompted questions about Whataburger's origins and why the combination tasted so good, but the more memorable part was the reflection in the car: when money was scarce, every choice had been an attempt to maximize leverage at minimal cost. Now he could picture buying things, traveling, spending time with friends, and getting paid to do the sales work he enjoyed. It was a small meal carrying a surprisingly large amount of relief.

Other Friday rabbit holes included why unusual jeans sizes exist and whether extended-size denim makes commercial sense. The range—from commission math and GTM architecture to burger appreciation and Steam storage arithmetic—felt appropriately Victor.
