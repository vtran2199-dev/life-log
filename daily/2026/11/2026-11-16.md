# November 16, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

After months of interviews, a disputed start date and an obstinate corporate Mac, Victor **actually started at Sparta Commodities** as the Americas SDR—then spent a packed first day meeting people, trying to understand the sales machine, nearly losing an Isaac meeting to a dead laptop, and learning that the job might take him to Barcelona in January.

## What Happened

### First day at Sparta: the job search becomes a job

Monday, November 16 was Victor's **first day working at Sparta**, reporting to **Isaac Musgrove**. That was a meaningful reversal from the previous week's attempt to postpone his start to November 23 because of equipment and IT readiness. He described having **about four meetings** during the day, including conversations with **Isaac and Antonio Castro**. The onboarding calendar also laid out a much wider network: sales leadership and AEs, **Ashley Brown** as SDR buddy, **Sam Barnes** in RevOps/enablement, HR, product and customer success, plus recurring one-on-ones and milestone reviews. The invitations showed the scope of the ramp, but not every listed introduction was confirmed completed Monday.

The pace was immediately overwhelming. Victor wanted to be useful quickly, not spend weeks collecting documents without knowing what to say to a trader. He focused on the practical sequence: understand Sparta's commodity-market buyers, learn HubSpot, study good SDR calls, pass the calling requirements and get ready to prospect. No new job interview, offer or rejection was established Monday; the career milestone was **starting the accepted role**.

### The MacBook dies in the middle of a meeting

The first-day hardware fiasco was almost comically timed. Victor's **LIONWEI side-mounted USB-C hub** occupied the ports next to—and physically blocked—the **MagSafe charger** on his 16-inch M5 Pro MacBook Pro. While speaking with Isaac, the computer ran out of power and died. Victor had to tell him he would be back in a moment. He tried charging through the right-side USB-C port, initially thought it was not working, then realized the battery was charging and had reached **80%**.

That led to a practical rethink of the workstation: two 49-inch ultrawides, Kinesis Dvorak keyboard, ELECOM trackball, webcam and a proper dock or charging arrangement that does not cut off power during calls. He discussed docking and charging with Antonio. This was a real failure and recovery, **not evidence that the long-running corporate enrollment problem had been permanently solved**.

Victor also explored making macOS's battery percentage and clock easier to see without looking all the way to the top-right menu bar. Native macOS menu-bar placement had limits; the customization was exploratory.

### Learning the SDR workflow through Claude and HubSpot

Victor began configuring **Claude's chat search and memory features** and using its connected company context to investigate HubSpot and other SDRs' call records. A **read-only audit** showed that his HubSpot account was present and calling functionality existed, but he had **zero assigned CRM records**. Other SDR call records were visible. Their AI summaries and notes could be retrieved, but **full transcript text was empty through the connector**, and access to recording audio was not initially verified.

The audit identified a handful of calls to study and a short **three-second outbound VOIP call under Victor's name to Isaac**, rather than evidence of a completed prospecting session. Victor began listening to a sample call and immediately noticed that the prospect was **already expecting the BDR's call**—a warm follow-up, not a convincing example of a difficult cold open. That distinction mattered because he wanted to learn what would actually help him book meetings. There was no verified production CRM automation, bulk prospect import or completed live outbound campaign Monday.

### Barcelona: a surprise trip, without a rushed booking

First-day onboarding also introduced travel for Sparta's **January 2027 GTM kickoff in Barcelona**. Victor had not flown since around 2023, and his prior work-travel experience was limited to NetSuite onboarding in Denver. The prospect of organizing international flights through **Navan**—on top of learning a new job—felt like one more urgent thing he was being asked to figure out immediately.

He reviewed the travel information and the optional **November 24 Navan training**. The January kickoff was described as **January 11–13**, with a broader travel window under discussion; **premium economy** was approved and hotel arrangements were being coordinated. But he did **not** book a flight Monday. Instead, he asked Isaac whether they could go through the booking together on **Wednesday, November 18**, and whether there was any deadline. Isaac replied almost immediately: **“Sure thing - we can do it together! All good.”** That small exchange defused much of the pressure.

### The human side of an overloaded day

The morning was not especially comfortable: Victor described **severe jaw pain**, sniffles, cold weather, and a cat in heat crowding his bed despite his cat allergy. He was trying to figure out why he felt so miserable before a day already packed with onboarding. The conversations do not establish a medical diagnosis.

Later, he also experimented with asking ChatGPT to use **real parallel subagents**, explicitly rejecting simulated agents and asking for actual start counts, identities and failure details. Those experiments were questions about tool capability, not evidence that a separate coding project shipped.
