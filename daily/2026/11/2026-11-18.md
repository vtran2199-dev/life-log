### Nikolas makes oil trading click—sort of

Victor had a substantial conversation with **Nikolas Plonski**, a former **Gent** trader in diesel, jet fuel, and related paper markets who had trialed Sparta and wanted his firm to purchase it. Victor later called this the **most tangible conversation of the week**, even though it made his brain hurt. Nikolas connected the software to the real question a physical trader asks: *Can I move a product from one market to another and still make money after freight and other costs?*

The key concept was the **arb**, or arbitrage opportunity. Sparta gathers scattered prices and market information, calculates comparative or landed economics, and helps desks discover opportunities that otherwise demand manual Excel work, broker messages, and WhatsApp conversations. A concrete illustration involved **42,000 metric tons of gasoil at roughly $1,172 per ton**, or about **$49.224 million in cargo value**. That is the value of the cargo, **not the trader's profit**; any illustrative margin was not an established average. The conversation also clarified **paper versus physical trading**, outright or **flat price** versus differentials, and why traders care so much about routes, freight, timing, and market spreads.

One discovery question stood out: **“How are you looking at ARBs now?”** Instead of selling a generic data dashboard, Victor could ask a trader how the desk currently finds and calculates those opportunities. The discussion produced real product understanding, **not a completed prospect call, sale, or trading transaction**. Some transcript terminology was imperfect, so exact technical labels remain worth verifying with Sparta's experts.
