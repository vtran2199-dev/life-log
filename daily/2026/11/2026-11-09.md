Victor objected: the agreed start was a real commitment, particularly after he had already canceled four other interview processes. He compared it to an employer insisting on an offer-acceptance deadline while treating its own start date as flexible.

Victor spoke directly with hiring manager **Isaac Musgrove**, who reassured him that the job was **“all yours, as we agreed.”** Isaac said the laptop was the obstacle and was seeking CFO/HR approval for **paid onboarding beginning November 16**, potentially using limited non-sensitive work on a personal computer until the company machine arrived. A concrete sign of progress arrived: Sparta placed a **Deel equipment order** on November 9 for a **16.2-inch Apple MacBook Pro with M5 Pro, 48 GB RAM and 1 TB storage**. Victor saw the investment as a strong sign the employer intended to bring him aboard, while still insisting that the start-date and paperwork promises needed to be honored.

### Impact Networking: a completed interview, not an offer

Victor went downtown and completed his **in-person Impact Networking interview with VP Nick Saenz**, ending around **3:10 p.m.** after running roughly ten minutes over. Nick declined recording the meeting. The conversation ranged from Victor's sales background to University of Houston and NFL football, and included an impromptu **CFO cold-call pitch** that Nick said he liked.

The interview also made the business model clearer. Impact sells outsourced IT, cybersecurity, infrastructure, hardware and software. Victor heard about roughly **1,200 Houston territory accounts**, an average contract value that had fallen from around **$35,000 to $20,000**, and AEs closing roughly **eight deals per year**. Meeting incentives were described as approximately **$150 for upper-management meetings** and **$75 for lower-management meetings**, while a separate **20% commission** claim was not clear enough to treat as a settled compensation term. The office was polished but surprisingly sparse, with Victor observing only about nine people present.

Nick left the door open, but further steps would involve a COO conversation and shadowing an SDR/BDR. That process could not readily outrun Victor's already-accepted Sparta offer. **No Impact offer or acceptance occurred.** The meeting served as a useful comparison: a real, traditional, field-oriented sales operation, but not an obvious reason to abandon Sparta.
