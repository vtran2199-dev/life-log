# November 4, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

Wednesday turned Sparta's long-negotiated SDR offer into a real $70K-base, $110K-OTE decision, brought a completed Agicap final and a fast-moving Boom onsite, and ended with an exhausted but strangely content Victor rediscovering Skate 3 and reflecting on the one skateboard trick he never mastered.

## What Happened

### Sparta: the call, the computer freeze, and the extra $5,000

The morning began with an absurdly stressful technical mishap. Victor wanted a clock visible near the middle of his enormous 49-inch monitor so he could track his **9–9:30 a.m.** Zoom conversation with **Isaac Musgrove**. A recommended time-display website froze the computer so thoroughly that the mouse and windows intermittently stopped responding. He scrambled to recover and made the call at the last second.

The call itself mattered enormously. Victor came armed with territory research and unusually pointed questions about Sparta Commodities' Americas SDR operation. He separated potential prospecting into **three buckets**: records already in HubSpot that an SDR can pursue, records in HubSpot that are restricted or owned by others, and qualified net-new accounts outside HubSpot. Isaac described roughly **600–700 U.S. CRM accounts**, **413 previously worked Americas opportunities**, and perhaps **200 recoverable prospects**, although the last figure was an estimate rather than a vetted prospect list. The conversation also covered actual SDR output, meeting progression, commission credit, and promotion. Isaac cited Ashley's **96 Stage-1 meetings versus a 60-meeting target**, roughly **78% Stage-2 progression**, Glendon's **15–20 meetings per month**, and Josh's **eight in the prior week**. Possible 2027 accelerators, including closed-won tiers or a quarterly multiplier, were still proposals, not approved pay terms. A BDM step was plausible; an AE move would require sales leadership approval, with a six-month exceptional scenario discussed but **12–18 months** a more realistic progression.

Victor pushed for a **$90K base**, continued pressing after initial resistance, and ultimately got Sparta's base raised from the earlier **$65K** to **$70K**. The revised written offer specified **$70K base plus $40K target variable ($110K OTE)**, **50 stock options**, **November 16 start**, medical/dental contributions described as **70% employer-paid**, up to a **5% 401(k) match**, and **two to three Houston office days**. Isaac extended the response deadline to **Friday, November 6 at 5 p.m. Central**. A further signing-bonus/additional-options request was rejected, and the proposed commission redesign remained unapproved. This was a **revised offer received**, not proof Victor signed it Wednesday. Multiview's already signed November 10 start remained the fallback.

The result was both a win and a frustration: Victor had squeezed out more guaranteed pay and gotten a much clearer look at the territory, but still saw the job as a potentially demanding SDR seat rather than an automatic promotion to AE. He was increasingly willing to accept Sparta unless a competing opportunity genuinely beat it.

### The emotional turning point after 1,100 applications

By evening, Victor described feeling completely **"pooped out"** after roughly **1,100 job applications**, dozens of interviews, relentless prospecting, negotiations, and side-project work. The urgency that had driven him for months seemed to have finally switched off. He felt unexpectedly **content**, with no desire to start another major software project or hunt down more companies. He noticed that caring less about impressing interviewers made him more candid: instead of performing for approval, he asked the hard questions and interrogated the opportunity. He imagined eventually channeling that energy into a high-output, "Wolf of Wall Street" corporate-sales mode, but for now the dominant feeling was relief and burnout.

## Discoveries, Friction, and Memorable Moments

- **Skate 3 nostalgia:** Victor played a free browser-based Skate 3 fan experience on his large-screen setup with an Xbox controller. The feel and visuals brought back the excitement of playing the 2010 game. He explored Carverton Quarry drops, odd test/black-world behavior, huge falls, the Coffin pose, and difficult flips. The game became less satisfying when he realized his old trick muscle memory was gone.
- **The one trick he never conquered:** Skate 3 led to a long reflection on real-life skateboarding. As a kid he could make the board flip under him but almost never land **both feet** on a kickflip or heelflip despite enormous practice. He wondered whether his older, more analytical brain could finally diagnose the problem. He also remembered longboarding powerslides and electric boards around the University of Houston, including a heavier board reaching around **28 mph**.
- **Hammer Jam obsession:** Victor marveled at Clash of Clans' extraordinary combination of **50% upgrade discounts and faster progress**, and wanted to understand how frequently such events recur. He was delighted by the efficiency and annoyed by how much slower normal upgrading feels. The event's exact end date was something he wanted verified, not a settled fact in the conversation.
- **AI-image-generation frustration:** He experimented with image-generation and outpainting services, was surprised by what some tools could produce, and expressed frustration with ChatGPT's stricter boundaries. This was exploration, not a shipped creative project.
- **A lesson in unnecessary tools:** The frozen-PC incident made an otherwise simple clock-placement problem into a near-missed career call. The practical need was just an unobtrusive, reliable time display, not another risky website.
