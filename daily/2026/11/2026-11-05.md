# November 5, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

Thursday was the day the months-long job hunt finally narrowed to one real destination: Victor chose Sparta, turned down Boom after an unusually candid and respectful debrief, learned Agicap's U.S. restructuring had killed its hiring opportunity, and felt the unfamiliar relief of having somewhere to land.

## What Happened

### Sparta wins, but the signature waits until Friday

After roughly 1,100 applications and an exhausting sequence of interviews, Victor settled on **Sparta Commodities** as his preferred next employer. The revised written SDR offer stood at **$70,000 base plus $40,000 target variable ($110,000 OTE)**, with **50 stock options** and a planned **November 16** start. The base had moved from an earlier $60K discussion to $65K and finally $70K through negotiation. The acceptance deadline was **Friday, November 6 at 5 p.m. Central**. As of Thursday night, he intended to sleep on it and sign Friday, then call **Isaac Musgrove** personally to hear his reaction and ask for useful training material before Isaac's vacation. **Choosing Sparta was not the same as having signed the revised agreement on November 5.**

Victor was drawn to the combination of sales execution, territory intelligence, and GTM engineering. He recognized the work from his Oracle NetSuite experience: a salesperson who can also build prospecting systems, enrich accounts, and reason about TAM is more capable than one who merely follows a call list. He also wanted a credible path toward AE, not years trapped in a high-activity SDR seat. The possible SDR-to-BDM-to-AE path was an opportunity, not a guaranteed promotion.

There was relief, but also a residual fear of something going wrong before onboarding and the first paycheck. He wanted to keep already scheduled Monday/Tuesday conversations as contingency rather than assume the job was operationally secured. He felt guilty about withdrawing from **Multiview**, whose November 10 start he had previously accepted as a fallback, and considered a considerate message to Hope. The exact timing of any formal Multiview withdrawal was not firmly established in the retrieved evidence.

### Boom: declining an opportunity without burning the bridge

Victor **declined Boom** before the proposed Austin onsite. He told **Mike** that the more he learned about the role, the less it fit him; the timing of competing offers was not the only reason. He also messaged **Jacob Walker**, whom he repeatedly described as his favorite part of the process and a leader he genuinely respected.

The follow-up conversation with Mike became an unusually frank postmortem. Victor explained why the roughly **$90K OTE** discussion, Austin relocation or five-day office expectation, frequent conferences, and uncertain career path were not compelling enough compared with Sparta's Houston-based, computer-centered selling work. He questioned the economics and productivity of event-heavy prospecting. Mike discussed the team's planned conference coverage and acknowledged the process had taken time. Both sides preserved goodwill, with the possibility of future networking help or reconnecting later. Victor was glad he could tell the truth without putting on an interview persona. **The Boom onsite did not happen.**

### Agicap: a strong final process undone by a leadership shakeup

A conversation with **Tracy** brought disappointing news from **Agicap**: a significant change in U.S. leadership had led to a hiring freeze or rethink of the Austin team's staffing needs. Tracy said they were not moving forward with additional hiring at that point and praised Victor's discovery skills and AE potential. He had completed the cold-call and final interview stages, but the company did **not** make him an offer. He interpreted the conversation as evidence that the obstacle was organizational uncertainty rather than a failure of his performance. The GADJIT discovery exercise had reinforced his ability to uncover actual operational problems without inventing a treasury-software need.

The two rival paths that had consumed so much attention were now effectively closed: Boom by Victor's choice, Agicap by the employer's restructuring. Sparta was no longer merely one bidder among several.

### The remaining funnel: Legion and loose ends

**Legion's Strategic Accounts SDR** opening resurfaced after Victor's October 22 application: recruiter **Laurie Page / The Bridge Group** invited a conversation. The remote role advertised **$70K–$80K base** plus unspecified bonus and equity, but no clear OTE. Victor looked at concerning Glassdoor feedback, especially poor AE reviews, and questioned whether another interview was worth the effort when he was so close to signing Sparta. This was an invitation and evaluation, **not a completed interview**. A Cintas sales-representative recruiting message also arrived, with advertised first-year earnings potential of $110K–$120K; no follow-through was established.

He also converted or reviewed text from several Boom case-study and presentation materials. No new JobCRM or GADJIT software deployment, code commit, or production fix was confirmed for Thursday, so none is claimed here.

## The Other Side of Thursday

After all that career intensity, Victor spent time reliving **Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III**. He had played **Danger Close** and **Operation 627** and wanted the unusual cinematic missions: AC-130 gunships, sniper sequences, tanks, vehicles, and memorable set pieces rather than ordinary shootouts. Zombies sounded fun with friends, but too time-consuming alone. A separate joke compared a Call of Duty character's appearance with **Marc Benioff**; Victor thought the resemblance was absurdly close apart from Benioff's fuller face and curlier hair. He also sought a playful dating-app response to a woman living in Florida, rejecting the obvious and pointless question of why she was there when Florida was her home.

The emotional shift was the day's real milestone. Victor had spent months in emergency job-hunting mode, aggressively building tools and interrogating employers. Now he could imagine having a career home. The relief did not erase the anxiety, but it finally made the future feel less like an endless series of applications and more like a concrete next chapter.

## Wins and Friction

- **Win:** Made a clear career choice with a substantially improved written Sparta offer, without confusing the planned Friday signature with a completed acceptance.
- **Win:** Declined Boom respectfully and gave Mike candid, useful feedback while protecting the relationship with Jacob.
- **Mixed:** Agicap's U.S. hiring change ended a promising process despite encouraging interview feedback.
- **Friction:** Still uneasy about contingencies, onboarding, withdrawing from Multiview, and whether to keep interviewing until Sparta is secure.
- **Memorable:** From negotiating six-figure OTE to arguing that a Call of Duty character looks like Marc Benioff, Thursday had a particularly strange range.

## Decisions and Open Loops

- **Sparta:** Sign or otherwise resolve the revised offer before **November 6, 5 p.m. Central**; confirm acceptance, onboarding requirements, and November 16 start. Call Isaac after signing if appropriate, and request onboarding or training materials.
- **Multiview:** Close out the signed fallback respectfully and verify any withdrawal communication; do not assume a message was sent without evidence.
- **Remaining interviews:** Decide whether Monday/Tuesday backup conversations are worth keeping until Sparta's onboarding is secure.
- **Legion:** No need to pursue a long process without first clarifying OTE, quota attainment, and realistic career progression.
- **Personal:** Enjoy the relief of having made a choice rather than manufacture another major project out of habit.
