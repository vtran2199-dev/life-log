## What Happened

### Sparta: from getting hired to understanding the company

Victor dug into **Sparta Commodities' financing and investors**. He compared venture capital, private equity, investment banking and hedge funds, then researched **One Peak, FirstMark and Singular**, Sparta's announced **$42 million Series B in February 2025**, and the difference between an eventual Series C and an actual exit. Isaac had reportedly sounded optimistic about a possible future Series C, but **no Series C was confirmed**. Victor returned to the **50 stock options** in his offer, asking how the share count, strike price, vesting, dilution and exit preferences affect their value. The discussion clarified how little a raw option count says without the capitalization details. It was research, not a liquidity event or a change to his compensation.

He also questioned **Sparta's discoverability in AI answers**. Victor increasingly uses AI to answer questions and Google mainly to inspect images or raw results. He wondered whether this shift hurt **AteOrder** and whether commodity-trading buyers would hear about Sparta when asking AI for software recommendations. A preliminary review discussed Sparta's Leonidas positioning and competition for mentions alongside companies such as Kpler and Vortexa. A formal cross-model GEO audit was **proposed, not completed**.

One onboarding frustration became especially clear: Sparta had thrown roughly **330 pages of Atlassian/Confluence material** at him, much of it company-wide rather than SDR-specific. Victor initially disliked Atlassian outright, then recognized why engineering and project teams use it; his real complaint was the lack of a sequenced sales curriculum. He wanted the material that would help him understand buyers, products, objections, HubSpot and actual prospecting—not the entire company encyclopedia. There was **no new interview, offer, rejection or confirmed outbound sales milestone** on Saturday.
