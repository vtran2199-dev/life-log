# October 12, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

A day that began with the threat of an unfunded Dallas move ended with Multiview's October 13 start officially postponed to November 10, room to pursue better interviews, and a surprising amount of real software shipped: JobCRM rejection automation, a repaired engagement workflow, and an iPhone Clash Tracker widget.

## What Happened

### Multiview: the escape hatch became real

The most important development was not a new job offer. It was getting **time**. Victor had signed the original **$65,000-base Multiview SMB Growth Specialist** offer and was supposed to report for training in Irving on Tuesday, October 13. On Sunday he had been agonizing over a Houston-to-Dallas relocation he could barely fund, the on-site 7:30 AM–4:30 PM schedule, heavy outbound expectations, and whether the Multiview One product and sales environment were worth the disruption. The alternative was not yet another offer: **Cision's remote Account Executive role** was his preferred process, with other interviews still uncertain.

On Monday he asked recruiter **Hope Davis** about a later onboarding group because of relocation logistics. At **11:42 AM Central**, Hope offered **November 10, 2026** as the next start date. Victor **accepted by email**. At **11:57 AM**, Hope confirmed the team had been told he would **not attend October 13 training** and said a revised offer letter would arrive **Wednesday morning, October 14**. The new letter had **not been issued or signed** on October 12. Victor had **not started work**. The original signed offer remained historical evidence, while the November date was an email agreement awaiting updated paperwork.

The relief was immediate. The emergency drive, rushed lodging decision, and first-day conflict with interviews no longer had to happen the next morning. Victor kept Multiview as a **fallback**, not his dream destination, and could give Cision and other opportunities a fair chance before spending money to relocate. The distinction mattered: a delayed start was a genuine agreement with the employer, not a resignation, a new signed contract, or proof that a better offer would arrive.

He made the change durable in his **job-search** repository. Commit **b52961d** updated the Multiview company record, disqualification review, relocation/cash-flow notes, transition options, and research index. The records retained the unanswered questions about commission thresholds, call-volume KPIs, customer demand, and the actual quality of the AE experience instead of declaring the employer a scam.

### JobCRM got a real, scoped rejection-writing capability

The other major story was making rejection emails usable inside **JobCRM**, Victor's private job-search tool. After the previous day's painful Zoho recovery, he wanted a repeatable path to record a rejection with the **original email timestamp**, preserve source identity, and update the application without inventing a second CRM or trusting loose company-name matching.

A preflight identified **20 candidate rejection emails**: **14 confident matches**, **four uncertain about the role**, and **two unmatched**. Rather than write all 20 indiscriminately, the team built a narrowly scoped **record_rejections** command on JobCRM's existing MCP endpoint. It used a **separate write bearer credential**, preserving the read-only connection; source-event identity and idempotency prevented the same email from silently creating duplicate rejections. **Migration 0041** was applied and verified in production, and the approved application deployment reached **Vercel READY**. Commit **4080dc6** carried the implementation.

A bounded production test with **Airwallex** succeeded and was read back before scaling. The remaining **13 confident records** were then processed successfully. The recorded result was **14 of 14 confident rejection applications updated, zero failed writes**, with source IDs and original timestamps preserved. The six ambiguous items were **not force-matched**: **HighlightTA / Neon One, Resend, Runway, Actian, Datarails, and Absorb**. One suspected Neon One record appeared to have a HighlightTA naming issue, but that was not a license to guess. The confirmed emails could be marked read after verified writes, rather than treating email-read state as proof of CRM persistence.

Commit **3a371e8** added a reusable MCP-extension reference explaining how future narrow write commands should reuse the existing authenticated server, canonical domain mutation, test-first verification, and durable checkpoints. The point was not to sell JobCRM as a product. Victor explicitly reiterated that he built it **to get hired and eventually stop needing job-search software**.

### The engagement popup was redesigned, then a real database bug was found

Later, Victor wanted the manual **Update Engagement** popup to behave like a human event recorder instead of making him set every downstream field. The redesign added clear event choices, local date and time with click-only minute selection, an email/source selector, a stage-effect preview, and an optional note. Recruiter replies and interview signals could move an ordinary application to **Active Process** automatically; offers and rejections kept their respective protections; other events did not gratuitously change stages. Activity and stage changes were meant to save together, using the event's actual time. Commit **3071a25** pushed the UI and related logic, with focused tests and build checks reported passing.

A real save exposed a deeper failure: the manual path was using a **Neon HTTP connection that did not support the required transaction**, so the UI could look ready while the atomic write failed. The fix changed that path to a **transaction-capable Neon serverless Pool**, not the schema or the visible form. Commit **57777e8** was pushed; **11 regression tests passed**. On a **nonproduction verification branch**, Victor saved the **CSC Generation / Backcountry** rejection as **October 12, 8:16 PM Central**, source **Email**. The activity appeared in the timeline and the application moved from **Applied** to **Rejected**. A read-only database check confirmed the corresponding **October 13 01:16 UTC** event timestamp. **Production deployment of this final transaction fix was not confirmed by that verification alone.** The distinction between a working test branch, a pushed commit, and a live production save was the whole lesson.

There was also a small but telling developer-environment fix: commit **77df4bf** documented a Windows **Codex workspace ownership recovery** path for a recurring setup-refresh error. It was tooling repair, not a new JobCRM feature.

## Things Learned

- **A postponed start date is different from a signed revised offer.** Keep the email agreement, training cancellation, and pending paperwork distinct.
- A signed fallback can be valuable precisely because it buys time to evaluate a stronger-fit opportunity without pretending interviews are offers.
- For email automation, **source identity, original timestamp, idempotency, and read-back verification** matter more than a high raw processing count.
- When a save needs atomic database changes, a connection that cannot transact is the wrong tool regardless of how good the popup looks.
- A personal widget earns its place by answering **“busy, free, or check?”** at a glance, not by copying the entire application.
- **GitHub is the durable record; AI memory is a convenience layer** that can lag real decisions.
