### Subagents: the magic was durable handoffs, not unlimited workers

Victor explored why parallel AI workers could be remarkably productive **if they were given a structured environment**. A coordinator can assign bounded nonoverlapping packets; reviewers save results; a deterministic validator checks IDs, hashes, coverage, duplicates, and permitted values; valid rows survive even when a sibling fails or a session loses context. The practical insight was that **conversation output is not a durable checkpoint**.

An October 22 plan identified a concrete Recovery A failure: **100 reserved batch-11 identities** had no saved outcome rows even though reviewers had returned answers in conversation. Manual copying of hashes could also lose valid sibling results. The proposed repair used **one coordinator, up to three reviewers, packet-specific JSONL outputs, a deterministic validator, and Git checkpoints**, with a small synthetic nonproduction proof before scaling. The plan itself was read-only analysis; later recovery checkpoints show that the broader project progressed.

Commit **`db575da`** added permanent **`docs/rules/parallel-agent-workflows.md`** and routed it from `AGENTS.md`. The rules favor small, independent assignments, idempotent retries, preserved evidence, and human approval for production actions. They explicitly warn that more agents add cost and that a shared workspace is **not** a security boundary. Commit **`78c4dc4`** cleaned up the execution plans: the Greenhouse fallback runner moved to `completed/`; unfinished Contact CSV Import and the **uncertified Recovery A plan/ledger** moved to `paused/`; hiring-manager outreach targets stayed active. The cleanup did not retroactively certify Recovery A.
