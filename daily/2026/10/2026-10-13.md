## What Happened

### Two interviews, two very different sales jobs

With Multiview's original October 13 start postponed, Victor could attend the interviews that would otherwise have collided with his first day of training in Irving. The November 10 start had been accepted by email on October 12, with revised paperwork expected October 14; **he did not begin working at Multiview today**. It remained a fallback while he pursued roles that fit better.

At **11:30 AM Central**, he interviewed with **Vlad Blumen** about **Reflow's remote SDR role**, described at **$75,000 base and $110,000 OTE**. The call was a deep chronological review of Victor's background, but the more revealing part was the company's go-to-market uncertainty. Reflow had no established repeatable outbound playbook, settled ideal customer profile, clearly validated buyer, or reliable prospecting signal. **Collective** was a useful customer example, but its founder connection complicated the proof; another customer was not named. Victor could imagine starting with Collective-like workflows and expanding from evidence, but he was frustrated at being pressed for precise answers while the company could not provide a clear starting hypothesis. The job sounded less like executing a proven SDR motion and more like discovering the market itself. No hiring decision was given. He **accepted a Thursday meeting invitation with Andrew Ho**, Reflow's fractional sales leader, as the next conversation.

At **2:00 PM Central**, Victor completed his **Curri Outside Sales** recruiter screen with **Alex Reyes**. This one felt better in the moment: he could picture himself selling a concrete delivery service to construction-supply branches, and said the conversation went well. But the details mattered. The Houston-area role was **home-based field selling**, not stay-at-home remote work: roughly **9–12 branch visits per day**, often **four or five days a week**, with the goal of getting each location to place its first Curri delivery. After roughly **30 days**, activated accounts would move to an Account Manager. Discussed compensation was **$80,000 base / $120,000 OTE**, including **$75 per activated location** and **3% of billed delivery revenue for 12 months**, not lifetime residuals; about **75% attainment** was reported in the conversation, not independently verified. Territory density, driving burden, and mileage or vehicle reimbursement remained unanswered. Victor liked in-person selling and the possibility of warmer leads, yet worried the work was effectively **field BDR/customer activation** with weak progression toward the full-cycle AE work he wants. Additional interviews were possible, not yet completed.

### A busy inbox did not equal a clean pipeline

Victor reviewed a fresh batch of employer messages and tried to separate genuine new traction from activity in processes already underway. The day's review identified **five genuinely new positive companies**: **Elastic, Ema, Blockworks, Digi Security Systems, and Ideals**. UiPath and Reflow activity should not be counted as newly opened positive processes. The review also surfaced **at least nine negative email signals**, with a possible additional WithMe case already represented elsewhere. These were **email-signal counts**, not proof that every corresponding application had been safely updated.

The ambiguity was concrete. **Osmind** had both AE SMB and Founding BDR possibilities, while **Circuit** had CSM and BDR possibilities; their generic rejection emails could not safely identify the correct application. Those records were left **Applied**, with no invented notes or stage changes. A **WithMe** rejection was found in Spam and had already been recorded correctly. Victor wanted the system to record real employer responses without silently attaching them to the wrong role.

That turned into a **JobCRM** improvement discussion rather than a reckless bulk write. **GitHub issue #14** gained a proposal for company-level ambiguous rejections and a general-purpose `create_activity` MCP capability; a **TickTick review task for October 14** was set. The broader priority list put **ATS refresh first**, then quick positive/rejected logging, full job-description context, workspace conveniences, and later metrics and cleanup. A priority list was not the same as shipping those features.

## Things Learned

- **A concrete product can feel easier to sell without making the job an AE job.** Ask who closes, who owns the customer, and when the account is handed off.
- A startup's lack of an ICP or outbound playbook is not automatically a dealbreaker, but it changes the job from execution to experimentation.
- **A new positive company, an email reply, and an advanced application stage are different facts.**
- **Pushed code is not deployed code, and deployed code is not a verified phone workflow.**
- When a local tool setup has worked before, reuse that known path before changing credentials, dependencies, or architecture.
- Buying time through the Multiview postponement made it easier to compare opportunities on fit rather than panic.
