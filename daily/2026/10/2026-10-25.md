# October 25, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

Sunday was the day Victor finally **stopped collecting hiring posts and started treating Outbound as a real sales queue**: JobCRM's bounded search operation closed with 258 actionable people, he cleaned up expensive Neon test branches, moved GADJIT from a disposable camera-deal mock toward a documented product, and spent the rest of the day wrestling with Clash widgets, an uncooperative lawnmower, LinkedIn oddities, and the strange generosity of Codex usage resets.

## What Happened

### JobCRM: enough research—now contact the right people

Victor's largest workstream was the hiring-post search and person-review operation that had begun days earlier. Saturday's **33-company batch and 47 active prospects** were the previous day's milestones, not today's new work. On Sunday he kept a serialized **Apify** collection running while independent coding-agent workers reviewed distinct saved-post packets. They used durable assignments, validation and import receipts so a context reset would not erase completed work. Intermediate checkpoints showed the Queue moving through **61, 84 and 87 actionable people** as more reviews were imported. The provider callback, request-control and shared-budget changes were reported deployed, including commits **`266b8d3`** and **`31f400d`**.

By the final saved production checkpoint, the three reconciled requests covered **315 distinct company rows**: **253 complete or zero-result**, **23 partial with possible truncation**, and **39 held for identity review**. Across those requests were **592 saved paid attempts**, **719 linked canonical posts**, and **605 distinct author profile URLs** in the read-only aggregate. **596 prospects had review decisions** linked to that evidence: **34 Priority, 210 Fallback, and 352 Not Relevant**. These are scoped operation counts; they are **not** 315 universally covered employers or 719 verified current AE openings, and the reviewed profiles are not all new people.

The final eleven-person review packet produced **five Fallback, five Not Relevant, and one unresolved identity hold**. Ten resolved decisions imported successfully, and the actionable **Outbound Queue finished at 258 people**: **204 To Contact, 44 Connection Requested, and 10 Connected**. The user also described **54 invitations in total**, with **eight acceptances that day and two from the previous night**. The numbers describe recorded states, **not 258 people contacted or 10 conversations completed**. No outreach activity was created by the final review import itself.

Costs finally had defensible labels. The operation ledger reported **$1.81510 of provider-attempt usage**, while the authenticated Apify account showed **$2.91 of $10 used** across the account; neither was a settled invoice or proof that the full account usage belonged to this operation. The existing shared authorization was **$8.29**, with **$0 outstanding maximum reservation** at the final checkpoint. An alarming **$14.80 historical sum of run ceilings** was *not* current liability. Victor had little appetite for another expensive sweep just to chase ambiguous identities or posts cut off by provider result limits.

The decisive moment was saying **stop**. The active provider inventory was drained, and the **23 partial / 39 held** rows were deliberately preserved as limits rather than retried blindly. Victor wanted to work the people already found, organized **by employer**, with a clear decision-maker, the strongest hiring evidence, application/rejection warnings, and an obvious requested → connected → messaged path. The next idea was **requisition-specific LinkedIn sourcing**, but only after the daily Outbound experience worked. Previously annoying navigation problems—jumping to the top, refresh returning to Discovery, and the wrong default tab—had already been fixed; they should not be reopened as new defects.

He made the lessons durable in a **documentation-only retrospective**, committed and pushed as **`0ab156b3985d50c1281871e25bc7fe854da17558`**. It covered provider state, cross-request budget accounting, identity holds, partial results, browser-session confusion, and checkpointed parallel review. It did **not** authorize new spending or constitute another production deployment.

### The LinkedIn experiment became an actual outbound funnel

This was no longer just reading hiring-manager posts. Victor checked the connection pipeline and discussed how many invitations were pending or accepted. He cared more about **which company had been approached** than about maximizing a raw list of 258 people. Contacting multiple people at the same employer without seeing existing outreach could look careless; one good decision-maker should generally come first, with another person as a considered fallback.

The day also sparked a bigger question: **who uses LinkedIn besides salespeople and recruiters?** Victor reasoned through engineers, managers, executives, professional visibility, hiring, and networking, reflecting on how little he had used his own profile for years. A newly minted **“GTM Engineer”** profile advertising AI-powered outbound systems and simultaneously marked **Open to Work** after roughly one month on the job looked absurdly fast-moving. He questioned whether the title represented a genuinely new career path or another trend-driven label. The profile observation was real; the person's motives were not established.

No **October 25 completed interview, offer, or new rejection** was confirmed in the recovered career evidence. **Adaptive Home Health and Agicap** conversations were still associated with **October 26**, not completed today. The signed **Multiview November 10** start remained background/fallback context.

### Neon: fourteen obsolete test branches gone, with real recovery evidence

Victor investigated why **Neon** was charging for extra database branches and untangled a persistent confusion between **Neon database branches**, **Git branches**, and **local Git worktrees**. An early read-only audit and cleanup plan said **nothing had yet been deleted**; that was a pre-action snapshot, not the final outcome.

The later documented cleanup began with **21 child branches**, 12 above the included nine-child allowance. Three obsolete Lever acceptance branches were removed first. Eleven more September/SmartRecruiters/rollback branches were selected only after dependencies were checked and private **`pg_dump --no-owner --no-acl`** backups were created outside Git. All eleven dumps passed **SHA-256 checksum and full local PostgreSQL restore checks** before those branches were deleted. Production answered a live **`SELECT 1`** afterward. The verified final inventory was **seven child branches**, below the allowance, with backups preserved; other Neon projects and Git worktrees were untouched.

The billing screen had shown **$7.61 extra-branch charges, $7.03 compute, and $0.09 storage/snapshots**, totaling **$14.73** so far in the October 4–November 1 period. Cleaning branches addressed future branch-count exposure; it did **not** refund old charges or eliminate compute costs. The restore dumps also do not preserve original ownership/grants, a limitation recorded in the docs. The cleanup and lifecycle guidance were documented in pushed commit **`c40486f`**. This was one of the day's clearest examples of **back up, verify, then delete** rather than casually throwing away old work.

## Friction / Lows

- The widget's finish-time redesign introduced a **free-builder priority regression**; phone-side Scriptable updates still required manual copying.

## Things Learned / Decisions

- **Stop when the marginal search value collapses.** Preserve partials and identity holds; work the verified people already found.
- **The employer is the unit of outreach.** Show prior contact, application/rejection status, the best decision-maker, and strongest post before messaging another person.
- **Neon branches cost money even when idle or archived.** Verify recoverability and dependencies, then clean them up; do not confuse them with Git worktrees.
- **A hosted app with a database is not live sourcing.** GADJIT's eBay access, actual inventory economics, and buying decisions still need validation.
- **The smallest fix can be right and still regress another path.** The Clash widget needs free-builder priority preserved alongside the new calendar layout.
- **A workflow must respect the user's actual context.** Sending the phone to Home Screen to hide an indicator is not a fix when the user wants to stay in their current app.
