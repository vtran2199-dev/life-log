## What Happened

### Sparta Commodities: the final peer interview actually happened

The day's first major interview was **Sparta Commodities' final AE peer
conversation with Matt Kelly**, planned for **1:00 PM Central** and
completed that afternoon. This was not another round of preparation.
Victor had already completed the Americas SDR territory exercise and met
with Isaac Musgrove, Shane Pratt, and CRO Jacob in earlier stages.
Matt's role was to judge how Victor would work with an AE, not to repeat
the entire sales presentation.

Matt pressed on the reality of getting useful meetings in a specialized
commodities market. A generic marketing-style SDR motion was not enough;
the seller needed to know the account, reach a real trader or
decision-maker, qualify the opportunity, and avoid flooding an AE's
calendar with empty meetings. Matt mentioned **Valero** as an example of
a relevant target and described the prior SDR approach as **"very kind
of marketing."** Victor appreciated the practical discussion but worried
about Sparta's relatively small addressable market, a target around **10
meetings per quarter**, and whether an SDR could exhaust the obvious
trading desks without a replenishment strategy. He also wanted to know
how credible the SDR-to-AE path really was rather than assume a
guaranteed promotion timeline.

The result was genuinely encouraging. Matt told Victor, **"I would
recommend that we move forward with you,"** and said he thought Victor
had a good shot at succeeding. Matt also explicitly said the final
decision belonged to **Jacob and Shane**. It was a strong
recommendation, **not an offer or a confirmed hiring decision**. Victor
came away feeling confident but still had to get through references and
wait for leadership.

### GADJIT: real business, prototype software, and rules worth preserving

Later in the day Victor returned to **GADJIT Deal Lab**, the camera and
lens deal-finding software he was building around a friend's real resale
operation. He discussed a business with roughly **16 employees**, a
reported **approximately \$100,000 revenue month**, and thousands of
camera/lens models in its sourcing universe. Those figures were
**user-reported operating context, not audited financials**. The
ambition was to find worthwhile eBay and other marketplace inventory,
remember purchasing decisions and buying history, and avoid pretending a
demo dashboard was real production data.

A repo inspection clarified the current state: the Deal Finder prototype
functioned with a **mock listing provider**, the Insights dashboard
displayed **fictional/demo financial data**, the eBay provider was still
a **placeholder without live API calls**, and **Neon persistence was not
implemented**. A working UI and a READY Vercel deployment did not prove
a functioning real-data buying workflow. eBay API/compliance
requirements and durable purchase-history design still had to be
resolved.

Victor wanted to transplant JobCRM's hard-earned operating lessons
without importing pointless complexity. The resulting documentation
update created **`ARCHITECTURE.md`** and **`RULES.md`**, updated the
project's other governing docs, and was **committed and pushed as
`a3a310ee8198f896ef592f06adbdbb06763d6ff5`**. It introduced evidence
levels, contradiction handling, canonical mutation discipline,
operator-path checks, recovery rules, a production database
compatibility gate, and mandatory **"Files read this turn"** proof for
substantial technical work. A subsequent review found no material
documentation error. **This was a documentation/rules shipment, not a
new eBay integration, database, or real purchasing feature.**

The point was not to build a miniature bureaucracy. Victor had already
lived through enough JobCRM migration and agent mistakes to want a
simple, observable path from actual listings to real buying decisions,
with the system remembering what happened.

## Decisions / Things Learned

-   **Strong interview praise is evidence, not an offer.** Matt's
    recommendation and Conor/Mickaël's feedback were valuable, but
    references, final interviews, written terms, and actual decisions
    still mattered.
-   **References should reflect actual working relationships.** A senior
    title alone is not proof someone directly supervised Victor or can
    credibly speak to his performance.
-   **The useful measure of a sales exercise is whether it advances a
    real process.** Boom's submitted assignment and booked meeting
    mattered more than another round of formatting.
-   **Don't create a whole new sourcing system to solve a short
    deadline.** The faster route may be finishing Sparta, Agicap, and
    Boom, rather than another broad paid Enterprise BDR search.
-   **Keep the signed Multiview fallback intact until a genuinely better
    written alternative exists.** The start date is a constraint, not
    evidence that a new employer can compress every hiring stage into a
    few days.
-   **A deployed prototype is not a production buying workflow.** GADJIT
    needs real marketplace permissions, accurate source data, durable
    state, and observable purchasing decisions before it can be trusted.
-   **AI should do research and preparation, not fabricate certainty.**
    That applies equally to prospect pain, reference reporting lines,
    financial data, and promises about job offers.
