# October 10, 2026

With an accepted Multiview sales job starting Tuesday, Victor raced to figure out where he would live and what the work would really be like, shipped more JobCRM SmartRecruiters improvements, documented his career priorities, and still found time for Clash of Clans, UI experiments, and several spirited arguments with AI.

## What Happened

### Multiview: a real start date collides with real-world logistics

The **Multiview SMB Growth Specialist** offer remained accepted: **$65,000 base**, on site in **Irving, Texas**, starting **Tuesday, October 13**. Victor had completed his background-check, DocuSign and payroll/onboarding steps, and was now facing the less glamorous implications of saying yes: a move from the Houston area to Dallas–Fort Worth, transportation, temporary housing, and cash flow before a first paycheck.

He investigated Multiview's office at **7701 Las Colinas Ridge, Suite 800**, including the eighth-floor setting and how much privacy or visual separation employees might have while making calls. He investigated the **Multiview One** product and its sales motion instead of taking marketing copy at face value. When comparing the product interface with a family member's HighLevel account, he noticed striking similarities in navigation, icons, layout and styling. That was a firsthand visual observation, **not proof of a vendor or white-label relationship**.

The economics and fit questions sharpened. The role involved approximately **80–100 outbound calls a day**, an on-site **7:30 AM–4:30 PM** schedule, a recruiter-described **~$130,000 OTE**, and no relocation assistance. He questioned how persuasive the product was, how independently he could prospect, and whether the actual compensation terms supported the headline. One modeled case, winning one new **$3,500/month** account each month, produced an estimated **$32,760 first-year commission**, or **$97,760 with base**, not $130,000; it was an illustrative calculation rather than verified attainment or the full signed commission-plan interpretation.

Victor was not treating the offer as a dream job. Its importance was that it supplied a secured income and sales ownership after a difficult job search. That made abandoning it for another *possible* opportunity a consequential gamble.

### The housing scramble

The immediate need was a furnished, flexible stay around Irving from **October 12 to November 12**, ideally roughly **$700–$1,200** for a private room, with a manageable commute, Wi-Fi, parking, laundry, kitchen access, and tolerable bathroom-sharing arrangements. Victor compared Airbnbs instead of committing prematurely to a lease.

Among the contenders were rooms around **$896, $1,016, $1,125 and $1,195**. One option, **“The Cozy Corner,”** had a strong **4.97 rating from 66 reviews** and a lockable room, but its exact location and commute needed verification. He also encountered a sobering **$2,115.44** all-in quote for a 31-night Extended Stay America room. At that price, convenience risked eating far too much of a $65,000 salary.

He contacted hosts and evaluated who else would live in each house, private versus shared bathrooms, parking, safety and possible extension. A host of a private-room option confirmed an attached bath, possible extension and several other household occupants. **A confirmed booking did not appear in the day's evidence.** The housing work was a genuine operational deadline, not merely browsing for fun.

Victor also modeled take-home pay, groceries, gasoline, and living costs. The car's brakes were an unresolved concern for a commute and move; do not confuse planning for a repair with having performed it on October 10.

### JobCRM SmartRecruiters: from giant scan to usable jobs

The SmartRecruiters provider work moved past the previous day's bulk scan. The larger inventory was now approximately **292,414 scanned**, **420 Targets**, but only **40 FULL records**, with **38 visible Target Jobs cards** at one reported checkpoint. Those are different stages of the funnel, not counts to add together.

Victor and Codex addressed **company-name confirmation** and the risk of incorrect automatic identity resolution, with an October 10 fix reported in commit **`0a56868d`**. Another deployed change, **`a53f035`**, excluded four unusually noisy or territory-heavy boards from bulk hydration: **xplor, abbvie, servicenow, quadient1**. Selective hydration created **22 new FULL jobs**, while **13 companies / 21 postings** remained behind a name-confirmation gate. Earlier on the same day, he checked smaller batches and production counts rather than simply trusting a green completion message.

He also worked through actual candidate review packets, importing SmartRecruiters Target Jobs into GPT for structured **JobHunt reviews** using immutable discovery IDs and source hashes. That turned ATS harvesting into something closer to actionable career decisions. A later count of **38 visible cards** was a subset of the **40 FULL records**, not another 38 jobs.

The lesson was scale with discipline: even nearly **300,000 scanned postings** produce a surprisingly small shortlist after title, relevance, company identity, geography and human review filters. Bigger catalog counts do not by themselves produce better job opportunities.

### Career direction: what should the next job compound into?

Multiview remained the only secured offer, while Victor kept **Cision's AE process** in high priority, with **Sparta**, **Reflow**, **JAMS**, **Boom**, and **Curri** at differing stages. No October 10 completed interview or fresh offer was established by the day's retrieval. Instead, he examined the choice itself: taking a less-than-perfect AE position now might help avoid another year or two in an SDR/BDR lane, but an ill-fitting company might impose relocation costs, rigid dialing targets, weak product differentiation and limited earning upside.

He considered **Sparta's roughly $60,000 base / $90,000 OTE BDR** structure, **Curri's outside-sales** work, and Cision as a potentially better AE outcome. He wanted actual closing ownership, autonomy, a product with explainable value, and permission to make AI-assisted prospecting an advantage rather than just a way to fill a call quota.

Much of this thinking was made durable in the **`job-search` GitHub repository**. October 10 work included a Multiview research workspace and compensation/sales-operation notes, the two practical documents **`relocation-and-cashflow.md`** and **`transition-and-options.md`** (commit **`2307ce7`**), a focused **`CAREER_MOTIVATION_AND_FIT.md`** that was integrated into career guidance (commit **`d5bef2873a69`**), and a dedicated **`interviews/companies/reflow.md`** brief (commit **`2a5ee8fe75b3a263260210ce2137147f3f483245`**).

The career-fit document distinguished verified résumé claims from motivations and hypotheses. That matters because knowing what someone *wants* to do is not the same as claiming they have already done it. Victor wanted a searchable, reusable record of why some kinds of sales environments energize him and others leave him feeling trapped.

## Things Learned

- Separate a **secured offer** from **possible alternatives** and a contractual title from the actual sales environment.
- Underwrite moving costs and take-home cash before treating an OTE number as money available for rent.
- A familiar-looking software UI is not enough evidence to assert that two products have a vendor relationship.
- Catalog scans, Targets, FULL hydration and visible cards are **distinct operational milestones**.
- Preserve meaningful job-search judgments in focused documentation, while keeping observed career history separate from preference and inference.
- Small verified tests beat multiplying retries when a scraping or automation method behaves badly.
