## What Happened

### Sparta: looking past the headline offer

The Sparta SDR offer from Friday was still not a signed agreement. The discussions dug into Sparta's proposed 2026 compensation mechanics: **$100 for Stage 1, $100 for Stage 2, and 1% of closed-won ARR**, plus a **possible conditional 3% arrangement** that was not established as a finalized guarantee. The options would vest over four years, but their economic value remained unknowable without the capitalization and strike-price details. Victor also examined a possible **second-half 2027 GTM Engineer path**, which was an opportunity discussed rather than a promised promotion. He reviewed Sparta's sales model, potential competitors such as Kpler, Vortexa, Bloomberg, Platts, and Argus, and the tradeoff between its hybrid office schedule and more demanding alternatives.

The bigger realization was about career identity. Victor wants to stop being trapped in repetitive prospecting, gain actual discovery and closing experience, and eventually combine **revenue ownership with GTM engineering and automation**. He reflected on his large-scale JobCRM search and data workflows as evidence that he can build leverage rather than merely make more calls. A hybrid closing-and-systems role sounded more appealing than a traditional SDR ladder, but it would need clear ownership and compensation rather than a vague title.

He deliberately did **not** send Isaac another Saturday message. The next meaningful step was to wait for the written terms and compare them with real alternatives, not negotiate against himself over the weekend.

### Agicap and Boom: a busy Monday and Tuesday ahead

Victor compared Sparta with **Agicap and Boom**, where he saw the possibility of stronger base pay, potentially around $80,000, and faster access to an AE track. These were expectations under evaluation, **not offers received on October 31**.

There was a brief Boom calendar scare: Victor thought the interviews might have disappeared, then discovered he had checked the wrong week. Both were still there. The practical tension was obvious: Sparta wanted an answer soon, Multiview was already signed, and the other two companies might still change the decision. Victor wanted enough time to evaluate actual written terms without risking the fallback.

### Halloween, Super Smash Bros., and the Joy-Con mystery

Victor dressed as **Jordan Belfort from *The Wolf of Wall Street*** for Halloween and brainstormed lines to introduce himself at a party. The movie's aggressive sales-bro energy made an especially fitting costume after a day spent dissecting sales compensation.

He also prepared to play **Super Smash Bros.** with a friend: Victor would use his Pro Controller, while the friend would use the two Joy-Cons. But the detached Joy-Cons seemed not to connect properly, even though they worked attached to the Switch. The console itself showed a full battery, while the controllers were nearly empty. After some troubleshooting, the likely explanation emerged: the Switch had sat **fully powered off in its dock for about a month**, and the Joy-Cons had not been charging as expected. That made a battery issue more plausible than broken controllers. Charging them with the console awake or asleep became the immediate fix to try; no completed game session was verified.

Later that evening, Victor worried about whether sharing a friend's vape could transmit herpes because the friend's girlfriend reportedly had it. He wanted a casual way to ask about the situation and tried to understand the difference between **HSV-1 and HSV-2**. This was a question about possible exposure, **not a confirmed infection**.
