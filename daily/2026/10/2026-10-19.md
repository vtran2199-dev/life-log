# October 19, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

Victor completed **four interviews in one day**—Ideals, Sparta Commodities, Blockworks, and Elastic—while his JobCRM hiring-post system progressed from cheap Apify experiments to a bounded, verified production workflow, and an oddly chaotic TRI-R recruiting exchange provided comic relief.

## What Happened

### Four interviews, four different versions of a sales career

Victor had an unusually packed Monday: **Ideals at 10:00 AM, Sparta Commodities at 11:30 AM, Blockworks at 2:30 PM, and Elastic at 3:30 PM Central**. These were not merely calendar holds: he later explicitly confirmed, **“I did 4 today.”** All four interviews were discussed after they happened. The contrasting sales motions made his preferences clearer than another hundred generic applications would have.

**Ideals — hiring-manager interview with Kaylee Parr.** Victor completed roughly an hour of discussion and cold-call roleplay for the Account Executive opportunity. He had practiced an objection-handling call involving a virtual data room and Goldman Sachs, working toward a tentative coffee or meeting commitment. Kaylee described a relationship-heavy field motion: approximately **20–25 assigned Houston-area accounts**, meeting people over coffee, lunches, dinners, and events, and waiting for unpredictable transactions. Possible next steps included a lunch-and-learn and a social interview, but **no advancement decision was confirmed**. Victor found the motion amusingly like **“Tinder dating with SaaS attached.”** He worried that so much entertainment and relationship cultivation, with a tiny account universe, would not build the repeatable full-cycle SaaS closing experience he wanted. His questions about hospitality/compliance were questions, not evidence of improper conduct.

**Sparta Commodities — final interview with CRO Jacob Koopmans.** Jacob said he saw **“no red flags”**, appreciated Victor's ambition and resilience, and explained that **Isaac Musgrove and Shane Pratt** would make the hiring decision. Victor found the conversation relaxed, almost a final checkbox. The apparent SDR target of about **10 meetings per quarter** surprised him compared with Boom's much higher activity/acceptance expectations; he did not independently verify that the two metrics were defined alike. Jacob discussed compensation elements around **$100 per meeting, $200 for stage progression, and 1% of closed deals**, and a possible move toward AE after **12–18 months, perhaps 24**, depending on performance and openings—not an automatic six-month promotion. Victor saw appeal in Houston/hybrid work and the specialized commodity market, but still wanted an actual closing path and an offer before deciding.

**Blockworks — second-round interview with Matthew Leightman and Brandon Slack.** This one was tougher. Brandon pressed on crypto-market familiarity, and Victor felt his lack of crypto-native experience showed even as he defended his sales and prospecting skills. The interviewers indicated that staying current and researching the space could address some of the knowledge gap. A possible later conversation with co-founder **Jason** remained dependent on the hiring decision; recruiter **Sadie Deens** was expected to follow up around **October 23**. No pass/fail decision was confirmed. The remote-oriented role's reported **$75,000 base / $110,000 OTE** remained attractive, but Victor questioned the product's value and the path to AE.

**Elastic — recruiter screen with Laila Samavat.** Victor completed the fourth conversation for an **Enterprise West SDR** opening, discussed around **$80,000–$90,000 OTE** (roughly **$63,000 base / $27,000 variable**) and **hybrid Austin** work. Laila responded positively and aimed to advance him to hiring manager **Alan Batista**, potentially October 21; that was **not a booked or completed second round** in the recovered evidence. Victor felt disengaged compared with more compelling opportunities and recalled having set NetSuite meetings that later became major closed deals. The company name and interview count mattered more than any imagined offer.

After the marathon, Victor repeatedly had to correct an assistant that failed to retrieve the four company names from his other conversations. The absurdity was hard to miss: a system tasked with remembering his day struggled with its biggest career milestone.

### JobCRM: a hiring-post experiment became a real, bounded product

The previous day's strategy was still **post first, person second**: find a recent LinkedIn post from someone actively hiring Account Executives, identify the author, verify relevance, and only then contact them. Generic manager searches were a fallback. On Monday, Victor tested a cheaper **HarvestAPI** post-search actor on Apify rather than blindly committing to an actor with a possible **$19/month** subscription requirement. The first Tekmetric test cost **$0.00205** and found an October 8 recruiter post but missed the known October 11 post. The subsequent **six-run Tekmetric/Dialpad benchmark** recovered **eight unique AE-related posts** at **$0.03830 provider-reported cost**. Keyword coverage was imperfect, but the small capped experiment was informative.

JobCRM then integrated **HarvestAPI as the primary hiring-post search provider** (implementation commit **`51e100a`**, checkpoint **`b3f3c89`**). Further work fixed Neon HTTP reservation/recovery issues, ran fixture-backed UI acceptance, and documented production release. Direct GitHub history confirms the day's implementation and acceptance commits, including **`66fca4526`**, **`b507b3b`**, **`ef50985`**, and later identity/reservation fixes. The live workflow now used **three separate hiring phrases**, a **90-day search window**, a **10-post per-phrase cap**, per-run and aggregate spend limits, company identity review, persistent run IDs, and deduplicated post evidence. It did **not** automatically send connection requests or messages.

A particularly important late result was **bounded production acceptance for a Dialpad Commercial AE opportunity**. The current JobCRM recovery checkpoint records a verified company identity, three real HarvestAPI phrase runs through the production UI, **20 returned URL instances deduplicated to nine persisted posts**, and a completed request still present after reload. Provider-reported spend was **$0.04020 including company resolution**, inside the saved **$0.10 batch approval**; actual invoice reconciliation was **not** established. Production Vercel was recorded Ready for commit **`d2beeef`**, with acceptance documented by **`a3a6ad7`**. Those Git commits were timestamped on the evening of **October 19 Central**, even though the repository checkpoint heading uses October 20. This proved a **small real production workflow**, not broad recall, every author's employment, every post's relevance, or a safe 300-company rollout. The nine posts still required human review.

The UI now defaulted to **25 companies**, could support up to **300** with explicit budget approval, and preserved search progress and review state. Victor also pushed documentation clarifying **OPERATOR READY** as a requirement for actual operator-facing acceptance, rather than treating a passing build or code push as the end of the job (commit **`a80b4e3`** late in the evening). He kept pressing assistants to say exactly which documents they had read and to take responsibility for completing authorized work without unnecessary handoffs. The next strategic question was how to improve signal quality and safely scale after the one-company proof, not whether to launch a huge paid batch immediately.

### Clash Tracker, AI frustration, and personal rabbit holes

Clash Tracker still frustrated Victor: the **Clock Tower's 10× boost** did not appear to animate the builder countdown the way he expected, and the **Discover** view lacked a search bar. He could not easily find a newly started **Freeze Spell** upgrade, and felt the catalog was missing a great deal of Clash content. A review pointed to an old pinned data snapshot and gaps around pets, equipment, and siege units. **No completed catalog fix was established today.** He also asked about Clash of Clans loot mechanics and what actually happens to resources during raids.

A glowing chrome bracelet caught his attention: it looked almost too good to be real, and he questioned whether the light effect was physical, battery-powered, or AI-generated. He disliked the return of **baggy pants**, preferring the slim fit he had consistently worn. He recalled deleting **Meta AI** after a previous session full of contradictions; that bad session occurred earlier, while today's discussion was about why the experience felt so poor.

One conversation became unexpectedly physical and philosophical. Victor experimented with **switching accents**—Australian, British, Indian, and others—and noticed that maintaining a different vocal “feeling” seemed to change his posture, thought process, and even sense of character without consciously planning each pronunciation. Another conversation revisited his past dedication to **remote viewing**: he had biked and run hundreds of miles, swum extensively, and pursued Ironman-like training **to improve his remote-viewing perception**, not because endurance sport itself was the goal. He felt the training helped perception more than accuracy, and he no longer trained that way after stepping away from remote viewing. These were **memories and reflections discussed today**, not a new October 19 Ironman or workout.

He also reflected on cryptocurrency's speculative appeal, joking about **“VictorCoin”** and asking what practical value ordinary users get from it—questions sharpened by the Blockworks interview. Through the day, the contrast between exciting tools and unimpressive execution kept recurring, whether the subject was Meta AI, recruiters, a game tracker, or assistants forgetting four interviews.

## Wins

- **Completed four interviews in one day:** Ideals, Sparta, Blockworks, and Elastic, with real feedback and sharper role-fit judgments.
- **Sparta's CRO reported no red flags**; Elastic's recruiter signaled a possible hiring-manager step, though neither was an offer.
- **Ten clearly matched rejections were recorded in JobCRM**, while six ambiguous ones were preserved for review instead of guessed.
- **HarvestAPI hiring-post discovery reached bounded production acceptance**, with nine durable Dialpad posts after a real three-phrase run and reload.
- Recovered useful new recruiting interest from **TRI-R** and **Impact Networking**, without pretending those future conversations had already occurred.

## Friction / Lows

- Ideals' small, relationship-heavy territory and Elastic's Austin SDR role felt less aligned with the repeatable closing career Victor wants.
- Blockworks exposed a crypto-knowledge gap, and Sparta's possible AE progression could take **12–24 months** and depend on openings.
- TRI-R's contradictory recruiter texts created needless confusion; Boom still had not followed up.
- Hiring-post results still required human relevance checks, and a successful one-company run was not proof of scalable recall.
- Even after four completed interviews, cross-chat retrieval initially struggled to name them all.

## Things Learned / Decisions

- **Four completed interviews are four completed interviews**: don't reduce them to scheduled events or let a missing chat obscure the day's biggest accomplishment.
- **Compare sales motions, not just titles or OTE**: territory size, relationship-building, quota definitions, mentor quality, closing ownership, location, and promotion gates all matter.
- **A bounded live acceptance is stronger than a code-only success**, but the results still need post-by-post human verification before paid scaling.
- **A rejection email must match the correct opportunity** before changing application state.
- Past athletic and remote-viewing experiences can be meaningful parts of today's reflection without being relabeled as activities newly completed today.
