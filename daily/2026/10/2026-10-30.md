## The Day in One Sentence

A real Sparta offer finally landed after months of searching, but the celebration came with a $65,000-base negotiation, a missed Stedi interview, several other companies moving, and a surprisingly productive look inside GADJIT's real software.

## What Happened

### Sparta: an offer at last

The biggest development was **Sparta Commodities offering Victor an SDR position** after a lengthy interview process that included a territory presentation, conversations with leadership, and references from former NetSuite colleagues. Josh Puckett spoke with Isaac Musgrove and reported that the reference conversation went well. Josh discussed Victor's technical presales and implementation experience and the AI-assisted NetSuite knowledge system they had worked on, reinforcing the idea that Victor could grow into an AE or technical GTM role.

Sparta's proposed terms were **$65,000 base, approximately $105,000 target OTE, 50 stock options, a hybrid Houston arrangement, and a November 16 start date**. Victor verbally accepted in principle, but the formal agreement was not yet signed. He expected further discussion and a revised package on Monday, November 2. He wanted more guaranteed base compensation and clarity on the path from SDR to BDM, AE, or GTM engineering. The offer was a major win, but he worried that accepting another SDR position could repeat the NetSuite experience of being stuck prospecting rather than closing. The **signed Multiview offer, with a November 10 start**, remained his fallback; Sparta did not replace it as a signed commitment today.

The emotional swing was intense. Victor celebrated having another offer and joked about the assistant's role in the process, calling himself **“victorPT.”** Then he immediately started comparing the upside against Boom, Agicap, and other opportunities. His long-term goal remained a closing AE role, with real discovery-call experience and the ability to use AI and systems work as leverage, not to become a permanently high-volume dialer.

### A missed Stedi screen and a crowded pipeline

While talking to Josh about the Sparta reference, Victor **forgot the scheduled 10:45 AM Central Stedi recruiter screen**. He initially brushed it off, then regretted missing a potentially fully remote BDR opportunity with reported **$110,000–$125,000 OTE**. He sent an apology and asked to reschedule, but no new time was confirmed by closeout. It was a frustrating consequence of juggling too many processes at once, especially on a day when another company was extending an offer.

**Redis advanced him to a 30-minute hiring-manager interview** despite the relatively unattractive $82,000–$85,000 OTE and four-day Austin office expectation discussed in the earlier recruiter screen. Victor submitted availability for November 2 and 3, but the interview itself had not happened or been confirmed on the calendar.

**SumUp recruiter Tyler Norris** responded after AnneMarie Ramsey's referral. The company was planning sales hiring for January 2027 and suggested reconnecting in December. Victor replied that he would like to learn more and invited Tyler to suggest times. This was a future opportunity, not an offer or completed interview.

**LTV.ai co-founder Asad Rehman** invited a weekend conversation about the unusual founding BDR/GTM opportunity involving Dubai, Las Vegas, six-day workweeks, and a $200,000-plus equity-inclusive OTE headline. Victor offered **Saturday morning, 8 AM to noon Central**, and asked for a calendar invitation. A call had not yet taken place. He remained acutely conscious of the clock: **Agicap's final round was Monday, November 2**, Boom was still active, and Multiview's signed November 10 start was approaching.

### GADJIT: moving from prototype to the actual operation

Work on the camera-resale business became more grounded. Victor and the assistant documented Tyler's real business discovery in the **GADJIT Deal Lab** repository, updating `PRODUCT.md` and adding `docs/research/2026-10-29-tyler-founder-discovery.md`. These were documentation changes based on the previous evening's meeting, not new customer discovery on Friday. No new application features or production deployment were verified.

The **Deal Lab** remained a prototype: its eBay provider was not integrated into the actual app, and Neon-backed persistence and live buying history were not implemented. Rather than building more mock UI, Victor focused on understanding the existing internal management software. Tyler's **GadjitManagementSystem** repository was cloned locally, and Victor created a **sanitized private snapshot** in `vtran2199-dev/gadjit-management-lab` so he could safely study the architecture separately from Tyler's original code. The reported snapshot commit was `cab9cbf746a8f6f6f4f8085ba2680c9fb1a54c2c`, with 117 files matching the remote tree. A read-only look at `README.md` and `app.py` began; no functional code changes, tests, or deployments were confirmed.

This also produced a moment of genuine confusion: there was now **Tyler's original repository, Victor's sanitized repository, and local copies**, and Victor wanted to know which one he could actually modify. The useful distinction was between safely experimenting on his own copy and leaving the owner's original production system alone.

### Tech, career identity, and the human side

Victor explored wearable computing and virtual reality. He liked the extremes: **minimalist smart glasses such as Even G2 or RayNeo iO**, or a fully immersive **Apple Vision Pro**, especially for dinosaurs, wildlife, sports, movies, and the sensation of being inside another world. What appealed to him was the feeling of a practical superpower, not bulky intermediate hardware.

He also reflected on his Kinesis/Dvorak keyboard, the speed of his old note-taking workflow, and how AI agents had changed what he needed to do manually. A past LinkedIn job-offer post with roughly **6,000 likes and 200,000 views** still stung because the hoped-for AE advancement never materialized. He wanted to prove he could move beyond the BDR title. His ideal next move would put him close enough to AEs to shadow, co-lead, and eventually run discovery calls and close deals. He was even willing to work in an office if it improved access to the people and experience needed to advance.

The broader feeling was relief mixed with overload: **two offers in hand or verbally agreed, several live hiring conversations, and a genuine choice about the next chapter**, but also a calendar and inbox difficult to keep under control.

- Sparta's **$105K OTE is not a $105K guaranteed salary**; the $65K base, commission mechanics, equity terms, and promotion path require scrutiny.
- A verbal yes is not a signed contract. Multiview remained the signed fallback, while Sparta's paperwork and negotiation were pending.
- GADJIT's mock Deal Lab and Tyler's real management application are different systems; understanding the latter is more valuable than another decorative dashboard.
- A high-profile public job-search story can be motivating and embarrassing at the same time, especially when the next role does not yet carry the AE title.
