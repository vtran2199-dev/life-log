# October 5, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

October 5 was the day Victor ran an interview gauntlet, pushed several more companies into active interview stages, took Lever from a corrected production scan through semantic acceptance and its first tightly bounded hydration, and then—because apparently three interviews and 71,199 scanned listings were not enough for one Monday—started a system-wide résumé upgrade while carefully refusing to let overlapping JobCRM work destroy each other.

## What Happened

### The morning opened with two Apollo Solutions Consultant interviews

Victor woke around **7:22 AM Central** and prepared for back-to-back Apollo interviews:

- **9:15–9:45 AM:** Alexis Rodriguez
- **9:45–10:15 AM:** Jeff Black

The first call began with a very literal systems test. Victor's TV/HDMI setup failed just before the Alexis interview, forcing him to debug the display live, recover, and continue. It was an unusually on-brand opening for a Solutions Consultant process.

Alexis covered Victor's background, discovery, and demo experience. Jeff pushed harder on CRM judgment, prioritization, discovery, and how Victor would investigate something as specific as a customer's mouse problem rather than jumping straight to an answer.

Jeff said there was **"no red flag"**. He also made clear that some competencies would need tightening while other strengths were already present. Apollo was still comparing candidates for a possible role-play panel.

Victor sent Jeff a LinkedIn thank-you afterward. Jeff replied within roughly **two minutes**—not proof of advancement, but a good human signal at the end of a demanding morning.

### Homebase became the day's highest-value interview

At **1:00 PM Central**, Victor met with **Stephen August**, Senior Manager, Sales, for the Homebase Account Executive hiring-manager interview.

The operating model was almost comically different from Multiview:

- more than **90% inbound**
- roughly **$120 average sale**

He connected his NetSuite performance, prospecting workflow, and founder experience back to the role instead of hiding the awkward edges.

One résumé problem also surfaced in real life: Stephen read **"Independent Trader / Markets Research"** as if it were a company. That small misunderstanding later became the trigger for a much larger résumé-system review.

The selected New-board candidate was **global:virtualitics**.

The first attempt did exactly what the safety design required: it stopped at **Needs Name** and ingested nothing. After Victor confirmed the company as **Virtualitics, Inc.**, the bounded hydration completed:

- **10 current matches**
- **10 created**

- **0 unchanged**

The queue moved from **115 New / 18 Registered** to **114 New / 19 Registered**.

That was the first real proof that a purchased Lever feed could move through explicit company-name confirmation into canonical JobCRM ingestion without silently inventing identity.

The Registered-board dogfood and final confirmation of the ten Lever Target Jobs remained unfinished at the closeout boundary.

### Late local verification exposed an environment-trust problem

A separate late-night Lever verification path used a temporary branch, **lever-build3-verify-luna-2026-10-05**.

An authentic **global:patch** fixture was imported and scanned through the normal UI:

- **6 postings**
- **1 Target**
- scan accepted

Then the app showed **350 boards** even though the temporary catalog contained only **3 rows**. That made the runtime database target untrustworthy. The work stopped instead of pretending the UI proof was valid.

No hydration, confirmation, commit, push, production migration, or deployment was performed from that questionable environment.

This did not erase the accepted production v3 scan or Virtualitics result. It exposed a separate local/runtime verification problem that had to be resolved before the remaining Build 3 closeout could be trusted.

### A résumé misunderstanding turned into infrastructure work

Stephen's reading of **Independent Trader / Markets Research** as a company forced a useful question: if one hiring manager misunderstood the résumé, how many other recruiters or ATS passes might do the same?

Victor did not treat this as a one-line wording tweak. He mapped the whole system:

- AE, AM, SC, GTM, BDR/Hybrid, and CSM lanes
- generated PDFs
- the canonical job-search repository and documentation
- AteOrder website résumé surfaces
- Short.io links
- PostHog attribution
- JobCRM résumé selectors, snapshots, and tracked destinations
- deployment and cutover order

The résumé content refresh was committed and pushed as:

**0ac7d1f83b23c2a2b0592aa62a8fcaf9ac27ae8c**

The next infrastructure phase was implemented locally in JobCRM:

- generalized `resume_generation` beyond hard-coded Gen1/Gen2 assumptions
- future generic résumé-link registry entries
- Short.io provisioning workflow and script
- related engagement and tracking changes
- focused tests

The dry run found **14 Short.io paths FREE**, and **231 focused tests passed**.

Crucially, the rollout stopped at the correct boundary:

- no Short.io links created
- no `CURRENT_RESUME_GENERATION` cutover
- no generated artifacts changed
- no deployment
- no infrastructure commit or push

The JobCRM tree now intentionally contained both Lever/ATS work and résumé-infrastructure changes. Victor explicitly decided both sets of work must be preserved; a combined commit was acceptable if the final diff was coherent. The new practical rule was simple: **no concurrent writers against the shared JobCRM tree**.

### The day ended with operator truth still winning over completion theater

Across both Lever and résumés, the same principle kept reappearing:

- A scan is not accepted until its semantics and totals are accepted.
- A hydration is not proven until the resulting canonical jobs are visible.
- A browser test is not valid if the runtime database target is unclear.
- A résumé migration is not live merely because the infrastructure exists.
- A scheduled start date is not a settled life decision merely because onboarding email has begun.

The day's progress was enormous, but Victor repeatedly stopped at boundaries where the next click would have turned evidence into assumption.

## Things Learned

- Real-time technical recovery during an interview can itself demonstrate the calm debugging behavior an SC role needs.
- Homebase's high-inbound, fast-cycle sales motion is radically different from Multiview's environment.
- Ambiguous self-employment labels can confuse a human hiring manager even when the experience itself is relevant.
- Provider scans need an explicit accepted snapshot; intermediate counts are not canonical truth.
- A **Needs Name** gate is useful friction when it prevents bad company identity from entering the CRM.
- Test success does not make browser verification trustworthy when the runtime database is ambiguous.
- Cross-repository résumé infrastructure needs a staged cutover, not a mass edit followed by hope.
- When multiple agents touch the same working tree, coordination becomes part of correctness.
