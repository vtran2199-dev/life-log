### Life Log went from a broken GitHub promise to a file-first fallback

The day's deliberately tiny **8:18 AM Markdown File Test** gave a useful, uncomfortable result: the real `markdown-test.md` file was created and attached successfully, while **GitHub publication failed a safety check** and remote verification returned **404**. That was **two successes out of four checks**, not a successful end-to-end publication. The important discovery was that a downloadable file could survive even when the GitHub write did not.

In the evening, Victor manually backfilled missing Life Log entries into GitHub: **October 11**, then **October 12 and 13**, through commits at roughly **6:36--6:39 PM Central**. He then revised the repository's daily-log instructions in three more commits to **require the downloadable Markdown before publication**, **preserve it on write failure**, and **finish with a three-to-five-item highlight reel**. At about **6:49 PM**, he created and enabled **one new Daily Life Log task** for **11:30 PM America/Chicago every day**, leaving the older failed tasks disabled. The objective was straightforward: get a durable daily record even if GitHub refuses the upload, without rebuilding the journal over and over.

These were real instruction and scheduling changes; they did not retroactively prove that the new nightly publication path would succeed. The first new scheduled closeout was due tonight.

### JobCRM: tighten the rules, then verify without touching data

Just after midnight, commit **`fe5b11b5`** refined **`CAGE.md`** so a coding assistant reads the current JobCRM rules at the **start of a technical task** and when the task or repository changes, rather than redundantly fetching the same files on every ordinary follow-up. It was another small correction to a recurring irritation: an assistant turning simple work into unnecessary steps.

Around **10:45 PM Central**, a one-time **Playwright** check navigated **JobCRM → PipeCal → the Multiview SMB Growth Specialist record**. The page could be reached, and **no record changes were made**. That was a read-only navigation test, not evidence of a new production deployment, a successful ATS refresh, or an employer-stage change. The larger idea of refreshing existing **Greenhouse, Ashby, and Lever** listings remained a future JobCRM effort rather than a feature shipped today.

### Clash of Clans: four times the space for *that*?

Victor dug into the **Super Troop** training options and realized that enabling a Super Troop did not mean he had automatically been training it. The **Super Wall Breaker** appeared to take **8 housing spaces** versus **2 for a normal Wall Breaker**. He compared the displayed figures of **78 damage / 35 hit points** for the super version against **66 damage / 82 hit points** for the normal one and was thoroughly unimpressed by the apparent trade-off. Those were his in-game observations, not a separately validated balance-sheet comparison of every ability.

He also considered unlocking the **Super Dragon**, but his regular Dragon was only **level 5**. Getting to the required **level 7** meant paying for level 6 first (shown at about **4.9 million elixir**) and then another upgrade. He questioned whether the resource and time cost was remotely worth it; **no Dragon upgrade or Super Dragon unlock was confirmed**. The day's most memorable gaming mood was essentially: *How does something take four times the space and look barely better?*

## Things Learned / Decisions

-   **A delivered DocuSign offer is not a signed offer.** Preserve the exact status, especially when the start date has already changed once.
-   **Create the journal file before trying GitHub.** A failed remote write should not erase the day's finished work.
-   **Test the narrow user-visible outcome.** A working navigation path is useful evidence, but it says nothing about deployments or background data mutations.
-   **Separate a game's displayed statistics from its full mechanics** before spending millions of elixir chasing an unlock.
