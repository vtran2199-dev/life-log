## What Happened

### Boom: the interview that changed the ambition

Victor **completed his interview with Jacob Walker, Boom's VP of Sales**. This was not another generic recruiter conversation. Jacob talked concretely about the outbound motion, market intelligence, account research, mentoring, and a path from BDR to AE. The discussed BDR role was around **$110,000 OTE**, with roughly **70% outbound work**, a target around **25 sales-accepted opportunities a month**, and a possible **6–12-month progression to AE**. AE earnings were described in the neighborhood of **$150,000–$200,000 uncapped**, but the exact compensation plan and promotion path were not independently verified promises.

Jacob reportedly told Victor, **“I'm honestly pretty sold.”** The next stage was a **take-home prospecting exercise and mock calls**, followed by a possible Austin onsite and conversations with the team/CEO. That was real forward movement, **not an offer**. The Austin office and travel expectations remained significant fit questions.

What mattered most was the chemistry. Victor saw in Jacob the kind of operator and teacher he had admired in Josh Hillman: someone who understood both the human sales conversation and the machinery behind it. He was energized by Jacob's candid, systems-building style and wanted to learn from him even though the job was technically a BDR role.

The conversation sparked the day's biggest personal realization. Victor stopped describing the end goal as merely getting an AE title. He wanted the **“AC-130” of sales**: a market-wide intelligence and execution system that could identify the right accounts, learn from every interaction, coordinate the ground game, and continuously improve. He said, **“I found it.”** The ambition became building and eventually commanding the revenue engine, perhaps growing toward sales leadership, rather than just occupying one seat inside it. His “sonar” and “whale” metaphors captured the same idea: see the whole market, find the best opportunities, and concentrate firepower intelligently.
