### Greenhouse: six hours to find new jobs, and too much old work along the way

The JobCRM day began with a bounded Greenhouse production trial that reportedly passed **5/5 boards**, then a full refresh of **6,837 boards**. Victor watched the operator screen and immediately wanted answers that were hard to see: how many boards had been checked, what remained, which job families were being found, and whether failures were recoverable. A **BAYADA** board stalled under a stale claim; recovery resumed the run without discarding its earlier completed work.

The final Greenhouse closeout reported **6,730 successful boards, 107 failed, and zero pending** after approximately **5 hours 41 minutes**. It found **126 genuinely new FULL Target jobs** (**56 AE, 27 AM, 18 SC, 9 GTM, 16 BDR**) and **3,291 new THIN Fallback records**. But it also **reprocessed about 21,665 existing job identities**. That was the painful distinction: the run finished, and it found real new jobs, yet it spent considerable time revisiting records Victor already had. Reprocessing is not automatically proof that every old application was modified, but it was not the new-jobs-only behavior he intended.

Victor restated the contract plainly: **discover genuinely new eligible jobs, compare identities early, fetch details only for new ones, keep existing applications and history alone, and show useful live progress**. A public Greenhouse listing API does not offer a practical universal “new since last scan” filter, but that does not require downloading every known job's details again; comparing lightweight IDs first is the more appropriate design. This was a design conclusion, **not a completed Greenhouse modernization**.

### Ashby: faster discovery, but a brutal handoff and UI gauntlet

Ashby became the main engineering saga. The agreed staged workflow was supposed to be **A1 audit → A2 approved plan → A3 scope approval → A4 implementation → A5 independent review → A6 acceptance/closeout**. Instead, the implementation handoff initially could not find the approved A1/A2 contract in a durable, usable location, so work stopped and had to be recovered. Checkpoints **`8867d4a`** and **`8aa5c3c`** preserved the contract and recovery anchor on the **`codex/ashby-discovery-refresh`** workstream. Victor was angry that a process designed to prevent mistakes was itself consuming time through missing artifacts, repeated prompts, and uncertain checkpoints.

The Ashby discovery approach was substantially faster than Greenhouse's brute-force revisit of old records. The reported full-catalog attempt covered **4,050 boards and 61,324 listings**: **3,937 boards completed, 113 failed**, with **493 Target, 1,473 Fallback, and 59,358 Hidden** classifications. The new records reported were **68 FULL Targets and 581 THIN Fallbacks**, while existing opportunities, applications, and interview history were reported preserved. The board queue was exhausted, **not error-free**; the 113 failed boards remained real exceptions. Victor contrasted this roughly **50-minute new-job-oriented approach** with Greenhouse's near-six-hour run.

Testing exposed an absurd operator problem: **UiPath was buried around page 75** of a 4,050-company list shown **50 at a time**, with no practical search or direct board selector. Codex paged through the list rather than first verifying that the test board was reachable. A search capability was subsequently added and verified. The focused **UiPath nonproduction check** produced **four new FULL jobs and one THIN**, with the expected Sales Engineer visible in Target Jobs; repeating the refresh created **zero duplicate records**. Dashboard work also moved toward bounded reads instead of expensive idle polling, with reported pushed checkpoints **`9a45201` / `088001e`** and UiPath repair checkpoints **`1f3b9287` / `dc9dd33`**. These specific checks did not erase the broader catalog's failed-board count.

A later **PAR recovery** attempt was deliberately contained: in nonproduction, **36 listings** classified as **2 Target, 1 Fallback, 33 Hidden**, then an identity-conflict guard stopped the retry. The reported test suite passed **90 tests / 280 assertions**; **`d5eaf12`** remained an **unpushed work-in-progress checkpoint**. There was **no verified production PAR retry or deployment**. The useful lesson was to stop safely on identity ambiguity, not to force a green-looking dashboard.

### The recurring problem was the operator experience, not just extraction

Victor had asked repeatedly for the ATS providers to work in the same recognizable way: consistent controls, progress counters, elapsed time, pause/resume/retry behavior, visible job-family results, and a clear distinction between **new discovery** and **refreshing old records**. Yet Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, and SmartRecruiters still exposed divergent control surfaces and polling/session behavior. The missing UiPath search was only the most vivid example. He described the repeated failures as especially infuriating because they forced him to spend hours supervising something that should have been understandable at a glance.

By late evening, the working assessment was that **Greenhouse's registered refresh should be treated as completed**, **Lever still had 105 hydration boards and needed a measured ramp/authorization**, and **SmartRecruiters was not finished**. A stale Ashby A1/A2 handoff document still needed historical placement rather than blind deletion. Those were **status and cleanup recommendations**, not evidence that every plan had already been moved, committed, or closed.

### A tiny review-payload typo caused an outsized debugging detour

Another JobHunt/JobCRM incident came down to copying an immutable identifier incorrectly. A review payload's **source-content hash was only 60 characters**, missing **`35ca`** from the expected 64-character value. The application correctly rejected the stale/mismatched hash; the error was in the assistant's generated payload, **not in Greenhouse or the importer**. Instead of checking the literal string first, earlier troubleshooting had blamed other possible causes and wasted time. Victor wanted the smallest correction and exact source identity, not a speculative architecture investigation.

That fit a larger worry about having language models produce huge **20–500-record review payloads** with IDs and hashes that must be copied perfectly. He discussed separating human/AI **FETCH versus SKIP judgments** from machine-controlled ID mapping and validation. He also revisited whether **Enterprise BDR** roles were being incorrectly excluded by a filter originally intended to avoid Enterprise AE roles. These were **review and design discussions**, not confirmed changes to the production filter or importer.

## Things Learned / Decisions

- **A completed recruiter screen, a scheduled hiring-manager round, and an offer are different states.** Blockworks and Digi happened today; Kaylee Parr's Ideals interview is next Monday.
- **“New jobs only” means identity comparison before expensive detail fetching** and no unsolicited reprocessing of old CRM records.
- A provider scan can exhaust its queue without succeeding on every board; failed-board counts must stay visible.
- **An operator UI is part of correctness.** A 4,050-company list without search can turn a simple verification into dozens of unnecessary clicks.
- **Validate immutable payload strings first**, before investigating the application or database.
- Use AI coding tools deliberately in future workplaces; security and intellectual-property boundaries matter as much as productivity.
