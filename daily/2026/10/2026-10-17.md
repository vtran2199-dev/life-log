## What Happened

### The job search shifted from applying to prospecting

Victor's application volume had reached a self-reported **roughly 1,050–1,100 applications**, and the available ATS listings were starting to feel depleted. He could keep grinding through application forms, but the central problem was clearer: **getting a decision-maker to take a chance on someone without a conventional multi-year AE closing résumé**. He wanted a real **SMB or mid-market Account Executive** seat, not an endless BDR detour. The signed **Multiview** offer, with its **November 10** start and Irving office requirement, was a time-sensitive fallback rather than proof the search was over.

He worked through a different plan: **find the sales leader who is actually hiring, especially one who has posted about an open AE role on LinkedIn, and contact that person directly**. The CRM already held a huge collection of company and job records. Instead of building another indiscriminate job scraper, he could use those records as an outbound prospect list. He considered Sales Navigator, Clay, Apify, LinkedIn post search, and contact enrichment to surface hiring managers, their posts, and possible phone/email routes. He preferred **manual, targeted LinkedIn messages** over mass automated messaging or elaborate email-domain infrastructure. He also did not want a rejected ATS application to erase a potentially worthwhile direct-outreach opportunity.

This was a **strategy and system-design decision**, not evidence that a mass hiring-manager outreach campaign had already been sent or that the proposed Apify-to-JobCRM integration existed. He recalled that prior recruiter outreach to about 20 companies had yielded around three conversations, including an ADP recruiter who connected him with a sales manager. That historical experience helped explain why bypassing an ATS felt worth trying. He also discussed how to keep JobCRM lean: record applications, interviews, responses and rejections there, while using individual GPT company conversations and durable GitHub summaries for deeper reasoning rather than turning the CRM into a transcript archive.

He filled or discussed application questions for companies including UpCodes, Base Power, Stedi, Kobie, Ethena and Skimmer, but the recovered conversations **do not establish that every form was submitted**. ### JobHunt review packets exposed an output-integrity problem

Victor spent substantial time processing JobHunt review batches against his canonical career criteria. The work was meant to sort real opportunities quickly, but some generated payloads contained **unknown Discovery IDs**, and one **20-review payload** failed validation at **REVIEW 14** because the importer could not recognize its required **Verdict, Operational priority and Reason** fields. He demanded a full, paste-ready repair rather than a speculative change to field order or invented identifiers.

The lasting rule was straightforward: **Discovery IDs and source-content hashes are copied exactly from the uploaded packet, paired correctly, and never reconstructed from memory**. A recommendation such as STRETCH APPLY is not proof that an application was submitted. The repeated formatting failures were a reminder that bulk AI output is only useful when it survives the receiving system's actual validation.

### A production rejection workflow, finally designed around ambiguity

A separate JobCRM problem was the need to record employer replies without opening a cumbersome edit modal or guessing which of several applications a generic rejection referred to. In an October 17 email batch, **58 of 59 messages were processed**: **56 confirmations**, **two role-matchable rejections (CharterUP and Mercury)**, and **one ambiguous Upshop rejection**. No new interview invitation or advancement was identified in that batch. Upshop had **three Solutions Consultant applications**, so a generic company rejection could not safely reject all three. Victor had manually rejected one Upshop application; the automated workflow was not supposed to overwrite that decision.

The proposed behavior distinguished an **exact-role rejection**, an **explicit all-role rejection**, and an **ambiguous company-level event** that preserves evidence **without changing any individual application stage**. A nonproduction implementation on branch `codex/activity-rejection-timeline` was reported at **`daa94c6`**, with the three scopes and idempotent saving tested. Later that evening the change reached production `main` as **`eda6634`**, with **migration 0056 applied**, **Vercel READY**, and live controls for **Log Rejection**, **Update Engagement**, and **Log Activity** visible. The live acceptance check did **not** save test records; full TypeScript verification was reported incomplete, so the successful deployment should not be mistaken for exhaustive validation.

Victor also approved a compact engagement time picker. Commit **`6554b42`** was reported deployed to production: the picker starts collapsed with a visible time, expands when clicked, and avoids an overly sensitive scroll-wheel interaction by default. The existing engagement/activity controls were preserved. He then had the coding rules adjusted so **small and medium sequential changes default to `main`**, while substantial or parallel work retains appropriate branch safeguards. That documentation change was pushed as **`914cb7e`**, with a clean tree and no deployment or production database action from the rules edit itself. Related TickTick work and the quick-rejection GitHub issue were marked complete, while **ambiguous-company counting and edge-case handling remained open**.

## Decisions / Things Learned

- **Treat the job search as outbound sales:** identify the actual hiring manager, use hiring posts as evidence of active demand, and personalize outreach rather than only applying through ATS forms.
- **Do not call a BDR promotion path real until its mechanics are clear:** quota, acceptance, inbound supply, activity levels, pay, and promotion criteria matter as much as a compelling manager.
- **Preserve exact identities and ambiguous evidence.** A generic rejection is not permission to change three applications, and a guessed Discovery ID is not a valid import key.
- **A production deployment, a smoke test and comprehensive validation are different milestones.** Record which actually happened.
- **Small systems should stay small:** keep JobCRM focused on durable career facts; use conversations and summaries for deeper reasoning.
