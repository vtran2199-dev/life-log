### The 815-post bug and the danger of AI review judgments

A particularly revealing JobCRM investigation found that an individual Opportunity page could display **"Show 815 more saved posts"**, including unrelated posts from other companies. The code had passed a **global Outbound evidence array** into an individual Opportunity detail card without filtering by Opportunity ID. That was a real UI scoping bug, not 817 hiring posts about one employer.

The review system had its own semantic errors. **Julia Schmidt's GotPhoto AE post** had been discovered and saved correctly, but a reviewer marked it **Not Relevant** because it misread a reference to the **Sabeba** team as a conflicting company identity. It treated an unverified employment relationship as a proven contradiction and suppressed a potentially useful referral from the active Outbound Queue. **Rob Burrell's Dropbox** review also borrowed the geography of Victor's saved US job application to support a separate post that appeared tied to **UKI/European sales**. Meanwhile, a **ConnectWise** employee's post about helping a friend find work and a **WorkWave** manager's post for a different role had been retained as cautious referral paths, not verified AE hiring-manager leads. AnneMarie's old SumUp post had been historically legitimate but was now stale for Houston. These distinctions mattered because bad classifications could hide the best person or send Victor to the wrong one.

A **read-only investigation** documented the examples in commit **`768e188`**. Then a **targeted repair** was implemented and reported deployed in **`f1bdda0`**, with documentation in **`4b6c894`**. The fix scoped Opportunity evidence correctly, improved full-post readability, clarified direct-hiring versus referral labels, and tightened the review prompt/tests so **unknown** did not silently become **irrelevant**. The coding report cited **37 tests with 143 assertions**, build, lint, and browser checks passing. No production contact decisions were bulk rewritten, and there was no new paid Apify search. The fix improved future presentation and judgment; it did **not** automatically rescue every historically misclassified person.

Later, documentation-only commits **`66ae854`** in **job-search** and **`b2a6772`** in **job-crm** preserved the LinkedIn research-to-message workflow and future company-level qualification direction. **JobCRM issue #29** remained open for later work. His boundary was the **"human bridge"**: automation stops where an actual relationship begins. He wanted natural language that he could genuinely say aloud, with no obvious AI-polished corporate phrasing and especially **no em dashes**.

The discussion turned hilarious when he asked for the most aggressively buzzword-filled outreach possible, a deliberately ridiculous pileup of digital transformation, AI infrastructure, data-mining refinements, and enterprise-sounding nonsense. The joke reinforced a serious product requirement: **AI may research and draft; Victor reviews, edits, and personally sends**.

## Things Learned / Decisions

- **The point of prospecting is a human conversation, not a giant queue.** Two real replies were more meaningful than hundreds of saved profiles.
- **A company's saved job location cannot prove a separate LinkedIn post's geography.** Distinguish the author's authority, the actual role, current vacancy, and referral potential.
- **Unknown is not the same as irrelevant.** Preserve potentially useful leads for review rather than silently suppressing them.
- **Keep the human bridge.** Automate research, code, CRM logging, and drafts, but never send outreach or replies without individual human approval.
- **An eBay developer welcome is not a production API acceptance test**, and a successful GitHub write is not verified until the remote content is read back.
- **Don't chase vague recruiters forever.** Real assignments and scheduled steps deserve attention; repeated promises without delivery do not.
