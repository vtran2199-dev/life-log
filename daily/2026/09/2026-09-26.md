# September 26, 2026

## The Day in One Sentence

A day spent pushing the job-search machine through more review and applications ended with an attempted Greenhouse data cleanup that correctly stopped before touching anything—and with Victor having to catch a Life Log entry that had been written too early to tell the day’s real story.

## What Happened

### The job search kept moving at high volume

Victor continued processing LinkedIn job-review batches and applying to selected roles. Several batches were handled across the evening; the surviving conversation context records application selections in multiple rounds, including a late round of five jobs. The exact total for September 26 is not established here, so this entry does not assign the day a single application count.

The search itself produced a useful warning. While looking for Solutions Consultant work, LinkedIn continued surfacing many Account Executive listings, even several pages into the results. Victor questioned whether the keyword search was actually narrowing the feed or whether he had exhausted the useful results for that query. He asked for the behavior to be researched, and a note was captured for future job-search reference.

### A cleanup plan hit its safety gate and stopped cleanly

The Greenhouse retention cleanup was ready for its first execution, but the required rollback branch could not be created because the Neon connection did not provide the project scope/ID needed for that operation. The cleanup therefore stopped before mutation.

The reported preflight showed 1,441 records in the starting B0 set and 14,078,456 logical B0 bytes. The five alignment checks were all zero. Protected groups included 517 linked records, 1,275 reviewed or stale records, zero operator-skipped records, and seven records with a null published date. No batches, records, discoveries, or source versions were deleted or demoted; the ending B0 count remained 1,441.

Victor pushed the related fix changes and asked what the next step should be. Ashby and Lever cleanup remained later work. The important result was not data removed; it was a bounded attempt that refused to proceed when the required recovery path was unavailable.

### Job search output created a sorting problem of its own

By late evening, Victor reported applying to 17, 19, 2, 5, and 9 from a review set and asked for the full CRM payload. Across the surrounding review conversations, he corrected which numbered jobs he meant and clarified when an item was already in JobCRM. That back-and-forth reflected how much selection was happening across repeated batches, and why the exact job identity and payload mattered.

The day’s work was still driven by the same basic loop—review, select, apply, record—but the search feed and the volume of numbered candidates made reliable tracking just as important as finding more listings.

### The Life Log entry had to be brought back onto the right day

The September 26 file had been created and revised in the early hours, before most of September 26 had happened. Its opening treated the previous day’s 163-application work session and the database-retention discussion as the main story of September 26. Those were useful prior-day context, but they did not describe the actual September 26 day.

Late at night, Victor flagged the mismatch and asked for a chronology audit rather than a blanket one-day filename shift. The audit confirmed that September 24 and 25 describe their own dates; only the September 26 entry needed repair. The file was rebuilt around events supported by September 26’s conversation context, without moving filenames or inventing an exact daily application total.

## Wins

- Continued reviewing job batches and applying to selected roles.
- Captured and investigated the mismatch between a Solutions Consultant search and the AE-heavy LinkedIn results.
- The Greenhouse cleanup honored its rollback prerequisite: it stopped before mutation when a Neon rollback branch could not be created.
- The chronology audit preserved the September 24 and September 25 entries and corrected the early September 26 closeout.

## Friction / Lows

- LinkedIn’s Solutions Consultant results continued to include many AE roles, making it unclear whether the search was useful or exhausted.
- The Greenhouse cleanup remained blocked on missing Neon project scope for rollback-branch creation.
- Repeated numbered job selections across review batches made it necessary to clarify which roles had actually been applied to.
- The early Life Log snapshot carried prior-day material forward and had to be corrected at the end of the day.

## Things Learned

- A cleanup that requires a rollback branch should stop before mutation if the branch cannot be created.
- Search keywords alone did not appear to keep LinkedIn’s Solutions Consultant results tightly aligned with the role family.
- Daily files need to reflect the local calendar date even when the prior day’s work is still being discussed.

## Decisions

- Keep September 24 and September 25 in place; their chronology is supported.
- Repair only the September 26 entry, using events that can be tied to September 26.
- Leave Ashby and Lever cleanup for later, after the Greenhouse blocker is resolved.

## Open Loops

- Continue with Ashby and Lever cleanup only after the relevant safety prerequisites are available.
